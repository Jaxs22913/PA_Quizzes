Landmarks You Can Feel (Lecture 17)

TermWhat you need to know
★ Sternal angleThe manubriosternal joint communicates with the SECOND rib: find the second rib for procedures, then palpate down and count.
★ Nipple lineCorresponds to the FIFTH intercostal space.
Fifth ribBelow the base of the fifth rib are the INFERIOR lobes; above it, the SUPERIOR lobes.
Jugular notchDeep to it is the TRACHEA.
LarynxC3 to C6 (the slide; the recording's "C9" does not exist). Cricothyroid ligament about 1 centimeter below the laryngeal prominence.
Neck trianglesPOSTERIOR triangle: subclavian vein and lung apices. CAROTID triangle: bifurcation of the common carotid. The sternocleidomastoid divides anterior from posterior.

The Normal Chest Film

TermWhat you need to know
First lineChest radiographs are the most common radiologic study (radiographs = 75% of images ordered) and almost always FIRST for a pulmonary or cardiac complaint; pain and shortness of breath.
★ Posteroanterior vs anteroposteriorPOSTEROANTERIOR = ambulatory, standing. ANTEROPOSTERIOR = supine, HEART MAGNIFIED: do not call it enlarged on that view alone.
★ Aortic knobThe rounded bulge at the top of the left mediastinal border (the aortic arch); pulmonary artery just below, then the left hilum.
★ Costophrenic anglesSharp Vs where each hemidiaphragm meets the chest wall: "nice and sharp" is normal. Gastric bubble under the left hemidiaphragm.
★ Heart sizeNo more than HALF the posteroanterior span of the chest; one third right of the midline, two thirds left.
★ HilaBisect the chest; mostly pulmonary arteries and veins (plus fat, nodes, bronchial walls); LEFT slightly HIGHER; concave, similar size and density.
★ TracheaSmooth radiopaque border, radiolucent inside, MIDLINE.
The rest of the readLungs radiolucent; retrosternal and retrocardiac spaces; only pulmonary arteries and veins as vascular markings; each rib one at a time, both sides; spinous processes midline. Lateral film: thoracic spine has a posterior arch.

Upper Airway Films

TermWhat you need to know
IndicationsMuffled voice; stridor (wheeze heard from the mouth without a stethoscope); suspected sinus infection; anterior neck pain.
Water's viewMaxillary and frontal sinuses, 45 degrees to the orbitomeatal line. For sinusitis: not really needed, but can confirm it and possibly REDUCE ANTIBIOTIC use.
★ Lateral soft-tissue neckTrachea = the dark, patent, radiolucent air column. The esophagus sits in the RETROTRACHEAL space and shows NO air: collapsed = NORMAL when not eating.
Bones on the neck filmSlight ANTERIOR curve (cervical lordosis). The body below C7 is partly hidden by the FIRST RIB.
How to read itSuperior to inferior, left to right; airways dark; sinuses air-filled; bones continuous; always symmetry.

Is It the Film or the Patient?

TermWhat you need to know
Good exposureVessels to at least the PERIPHERAL THIRD; paraspinous margins; left hemidiaphragm visible behind the heart.
OverexposedDARK: thoracic spine, mediastinum, retrocardiac areas and nasogastric/endotracheal tubes show through.
UnderexposedWHITE: small vessels, NO spine and NO mediastinal anatomy.
InspirationDomes to the TENTH posterior rib standing (eighth to tenth seated or lying). Few ribs above the diaphragm = poor effort.
★ RotationSpinous processes to the clavicle borders. Do NOT use the tracheal shadow (a tube looks the same rotated).
The checklistTrachea midline or deviated; lungs shadowing or lucency; vessels enlarged; HILA masses or lymphadenopathy; mediastinum width or mass; PLEURA effusion, thickening, calcification; BONES lesions or fractures; soft tissues: don't miss a mastectomy.

Choosing the Study

TermWhat you need to know
Computed tomography first?ONLY for a suspected pulmonary embolism: the computed tomography ANGIOGRAM. Everything else starts with the radiograph.
Radiograph indicationsChest pain, dyspnea, cough over 6 WEEKS unexplained, unexplained weight loss in any chronic SMOKER, hemoptysis, adventitious sounds, support hardware (lines, tubes, pacemaker).
Computed tomographyMost common reason: an INCONCLUSIVE radiograph with a continuing problem. Also embolism, trauma, chronic obstructive pulmonary and interstitial lung disease, BONE (rib and spine fractures), cancer screening. Head and neck: retropharyngeal abscess or foreign body the radiograph missed.
Contrast is forVASCULAR pathology (embolism, dissection, aneurysm), focal lesions, the -itis conditions (magnetic resonance superior for vasculitis), METASTASES to lymph nodes.
UltrasoundAlmost exclusively TRAUMA: collapsed lung or fluid (extended focused assessment with sonography for trauma); procedures; NO contraindications. Ribs cast shadows.
★ Ventilation/perfusionFor pulmonary embolism when the angiogram can't be done (CONTRAST ALLERGY); INFERIOR to the angiogram. Ventilation: INHALED technetium-99m. Perfusion: INJECTED technetium-99m macroaggregated albumin. Result: low, medium or high probability.
Positron emission tomographyHigher resolution and sensitivity than single-photon, more cost; usually secondary to the radiograph; mostly for METASTASES; now done with computed tomography for a much CLEARER image.
Magnetic resonanceKing of soft tissue, but the lungs' anatomy makes computed tomography first. Used for a mass INCONCLUSIVE on computed tomography, patients who can't have it, vasculopathy (second choice); SUPERIOR to the angiogram for INTRAMURAL HEMATOMA. Inflammation: dark T1, bright T2.
Who reads itThe radiologist gives the official read; the clinician's read is often just for the answer.

Contraindications and Cautions

TermWhat you need to know
★ RadiographsNO true contraindications. Lifetime limit 100 millisieverts = about 10,000 chest radiographs or 25 chest computed tomography scans.
★ Pregnancy test"Always, always, always": beta human chorionic gonadotropin (urine or blood) on every female of childbearing age. Pregnancy is NOT an absolute contraindication; emergent imaging is done, even with contrast.
Lead shieldingEverything not being filmed, especially thyroid, ovaries, testes, breasts, fetus.
★ Contrast cautionsAnaphylaxis to medicines; ★ SHELLFISH anaphylaxis not yet tested for iodine allergy; iodine anaphylaxis or angioedema; HIGH CREATININE or blood urea nitrogen (renally cleared, NEPHROTOXIC); suspected THYROID dysfunction.
Magnetic resonance precautionsMetal implants that are NOT titanium; gadolinium allergy (rare); claustrophobia.

Reading Computed Tomography

TermWhat you need to know
PlanesTRANSVERSE (axial) = cross-section; FRONTAL (coronal) = both lungs, apex to base; SAGITTAL = one side in profile. A slice, not a stacked shadow.
AttenuationHIGH (bright): bone (very high), FRESH blood, contrast. LOW (dark): air and gas, fluid, coagulated blood. NORMAL: heart and muscle.
Angiogram pearlsDESCENDING AORTA always on the LEFT of the patient's vertebral column; patient's left = YOUR right; RIGHT ventricle nearest the sternum; LEFT ventricle has the thickest wall; for an embolism the PULMONARY ARTERIES matter most.
ClotA dark filling defect inside a bright, contrast-filled pulmonary artery.
VasculogramAlmost always a computed tomography angiogram: embolism, pulmonary hypertension, vasculitis, scleroderma.

The Five Patterns

TermWhat you need to know
PneumoniaCONSOLIDATION (infiltrate): white, filled-in lung where it should be dark. Don't sort bacterial from viral on the film.
★ Pleural effusionBLUNTED costophrenic angles; on computed tomography a gray collection compressing the lung.
★ Pulmonary edemaCEPHALIZATION of vessels, KERLEY B LINES (short horizontal lines at the outer lower lungs from thickened INTERLOBULAR SEPTA), pleural effusion, INCREASED cardiothoracic ratio.
PneumothoraxJET BLACK air with the COMPRESSED lung beside it.
★ Interstitial lung diseaseDiffuse abnormal patterns in the INTERSTITIUM, not the air sacs. Computed tomography: reticular (net-like), nodular (dots), HONEYCOMBING (cysts 3 to 10 millimeters), volume loss, traction bronchiectasis, GROUND-GLASS (hazy, pale), Kerley B lines.
