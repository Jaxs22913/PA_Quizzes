Antiarrhythmic Drugs Compared by Class

Classes I to IV and the three miscellaneous agents side by side: what each blocks, what it does to the action potential (read off the slide pictures), what it does to conduction, what it is for, what it causes and what to avoid.

Lecture 10 — Antiarrhythmic Drugs 9 rows

Read the indications column for the trend. Class I and class III work on both supraventricular and ventricular arrhythmias; class II, class IV and the miscellaneous agents are listed for supraventricular arrhythmias (propranolol's slide adds ventricular ectopic beats, and verapamil and diltiazem are to be avoided in ventricular arrhythmias). Read the action potential column for the mechanism: class IA and IC slow phase 0 (IC the most), class IB shortens the action potential and works mainly in ischemic tissue, class III lengthens repolarization, and class II and IV flatten phase 4 in the pacemaker cells of the sinoatrial and atrioventricular nodes. Torsades de pointes belongs to the drugs that lengthen repolarization (class IA, dofetilide, sotalol) and is treated with magnesium sulfate. Doses are not tested and are left out.
