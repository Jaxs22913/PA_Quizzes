Tuberculosis Drugs Compared

The four first-line drugs and the second-line agents: whether each is bacteriostatic or bactericidal, what it targets, how it is used, its signature adverse effects, what to monitor and teach, and how it is eliminated or resisted. No doses.

Lecture 11 — Drugs for Pulmonary Infections, Asthma and Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease 6 rows

One signature per drug. Isoniazid: peripheral neuritis, prevented by pyridoxine. Rifampin: orange secretions and enzyme induction (it lowers contraceptives and anticoagulants). Ethambutol: red-green color vision. Pyrazinamide: hyperuricemia and hepatitis, and not in pregnancy. Three of the four can inflame the liver (isoniazid, rifampin, pyrazinamide). Isoniazid and ethambutol both act on the waxy mycolic acid coat; rifampin acts on RNA polymerase. The slides disagree with standard microbiology on one point: rifampin’s spectrum list puts Neisseria and Legionella among gram-positive organisms; both are gram-negative, and the list is not used here. Doses are not tested and are left out.
