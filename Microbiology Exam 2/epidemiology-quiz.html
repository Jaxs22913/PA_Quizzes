Before you begin

Lecture 12 of Microbiology — Epidemiology. Thirty questions, every one cited to the slide it came from, and every one of the ten objectives in each set. The two branches: descriptive epidemiology describes a disease by person, place and time and forms hypotheses (community health surveys); analytic epidemiology tests a hypothesis about an exposure and an outcome through host, agent and environment (cohort, case-control and experimental designs). Know who did what in the history (Hippocrates, Graunt, Farr, Snow, Doll and Hill) and what genomic epidemiology adds; the sources of data; surveillance (timeliness, representation, sensitivity, specificity; active, passive, sentinel, syndromic); what makes a disease reportable and how each group is reported; incidence versus prevalence and the mortality rates; association versus causation, the pyramid of associations and Hill's criteria; endemic, sporadic, epidemic, pandemic, the epidemic types, the reproduction numbers and herd immunity; and the applications, from the steps of an outbreak investigation to policy, social and behavioral, environmental and occupational work. No question asks for a year, a percentage or a case count.
