Before you begin

Lecture 11 of Microbiology — Novel Antimicrobial Therapy. Thirty questions, every one cited to the slide it came from. The problem: resistance follows use, driven by agricultural overuse, inappropriate prescribing and patients not finishing their courses; stewardship answers it with education and a cycle of diagnose, consider a non-antibiotic option, use antimicrobials only when needed, and re-evaluate. Know how antibiotics are chosen today (disk diffusion, antibiograms, empiric therapy started before results), when prophylaxis is justified, and which adverse effects are direct (allergy, toxicity, interactions, failure) and which indirect (Clostridioides difficile, resistant infection, environmental flora). The options: fecal microbiota transplant, screened like blood donation and given by nasogastric tube, colonoscopy or enema, for recurrent Clostridioides difficile; phage therapy, lytic phages so specific that the cause must be identified first, which die off once their host is gone; and the other alternatives (corticosteroids, donor antibodies, prebiotics and probiotics, a varied plant diet, plant essential oils). No question asks for a date or a percentage.
