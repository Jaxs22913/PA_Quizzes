Types of Pathogens

TermWhat you need to know
Prokaryotic vs eukaryoticPRO: no nuclear membrane, few organelles, circular naked DNA, peptidoglycan wall, 70S ribosomes. EU: nuclear membrane, many organelles, linear DNA + histones, carbohydrate wall, 80S ribosomes.
The five typesBacteria (prokaryotic) · fungi, protozoa, helminths (eukaryotic) · viruses (noncellular infectious agents).
Chlamydias vs RickettsiasBoth tiny obligate intracellular. Rickettsias ARE arthropod-transmitted (fleas, ticks, lice); Chlamydias are NOT.
MycoplasmasTiny, pleomorphic, LACK A CELL WALL. M. pneumoniae.
FungiYeasts unicellular (Candida albicans) · molds multicellular and filamentous (Penicillium notatum). Differentiated by spore type.
ProtozoaTrophozoite = motile feeding stage; cyst = dormant resistant stage. Mastigophora (flagellates), Sarcodina (amoebas), Ciliophora (ciliates), Apicomplexa (nonmotile — Plasmodium).
HelminthsFlatworms: no body cavity, blind-pouch gut — cestodes, trematodes. Roundworms: complete gut, cuticle, spines and hooks.
Prion vs viroidPRION = misfolded protein, NO nucleic acid → spongiform encephalopathy. VIROID = short RNA, NO protein coat, plants only. Exact opposites.

Bacterial Structures

TermWhat you need to know
FlagellumTrue directional motility.
PilusCoded by a PLASMID; used in CONJUGATION.
FimbriaeAdhesion to membrane surfaces.
GlycocalyxCapsule/slime layer. Biofilms · prevents desiccation · prevents phagocytosis (pathogenicity factor). S. pneumoniae.
PlasmidsSmall circular dsDNA, free or integrated, NOT essential. May encode antibiotic resistance, metal tolerance, enzymes, toxins.
EndosporesSurvival mechanism, resistant coat, very low metabolism. BACILLUS and CLOSTRIDIUM.

Morphology & Gram Stain

TermWhat you need to know
Three shapesCocci (spherical) · bacilli (rod) · spiral (helical, comma, twisted rod, spirochete). Matters for IDENTIFICATION.
PeptidoglycanAlternating N-acetyl glucosamine + N-acetyl muramic acid, crosslinked by a short peptide bridge. Crosslinking by TRANSPEPTIDASES = target of beta lactams.
Gram stain stepsCrystal violet 1min → iodine 1min → DECOLORIZE acetone alcohol ~10sec (MOST CRITICAL) → safranin 1min.
Gram positiveThick homogenous peptidoglycan 20-80nm + teichoic/lipoteichoic acid. RETAINS crystal violet → purple. PHYSICALLY strong (temp, pH, osmotic).
Gram negativeOuter membrane (LPS outermost) + periplasmic space + thin peptidoglycan + inner membrane. LOSES crystal violet → red. CHEMICALLY strong (disinfectants, antibiotics).
Lipopolysaccharide3 parts: LIPID A (endotoxin activity) · core polysaccharide · O-POLYSACCHARIDE (classification).

Identification & Media

TermWhat you need to know
6 identification methodsMicroscopic morphology · macroscopic (colony) morphology · physiological/biochemical · chemical · serological · genetic and molecular (G+C composition, probes, sequencing/rRNA).
Growth requirementsCHEMICAL: water, energy nutrients, vitamins, minerals, oxygen. PHYSICAL: temperature, pH, osmotic pressure.
Synthetic vs complexSynthetic = pure compounds, exact formula. Complex = ≥1 ingredient not chemically definable.
EnrichedBlood, serum, haemoglobin or growth factors for FASTIDIOUS microbes.
Selective vs differentialSELECTIVE inhibits some / encourages desired. DIFFERENTIAL lets several grow and SHOWS visible differences.
CombinationsBlood agar = enriched + differential (gamma/beta/alpha haemolysis). Mannitol salt agar = selective + differential.

Growth & Death Curves

TermWhat you need to know
Growth = ?Increase in NUMBER, not size. Binary fission → exponential, not arithmetic.
LagFlat period of adjustment and enlargement; little growth.
ExponentialMaximum growth while nutrients/environment allow. MOST VULNERABLE to control here — e.g. penicillin.
StationaryGrowth rate = death rate. Depleted nutrients and oxygen; organic acids and pollutants excreted.
DeathLimiting factors intensify; cells die exponentially in their own wastes.
Death curveRate of death under a control method is CONSTANT. Decimal reduction time = time to kill 90%. 4.9M → 486K → 48.6K → 4,860.

Viruses & Phages

TermWhat you need to know
All viruses haveCAPSIDS — protein coats enclosing the nucleic acid. Helical, icosahedral or complex. DNA or RNA. May have envelope or spikes.
6 phage stepsAdsorption → penetration → replication → assembly → maturation → release.
LyticRapid takeover, multiple copies, host destroyed. GENERALIZED transduction (random host DNA).
LysogenicIntegrates as PROPHAGE. May gain new traits (e.g. toxin production). Immune to reinfection. Replicated with host. SPECIALIZED transduction (same DNA in all cells).
Animal vs phageEntire virion ENGULFED · UNCOATING required (cytoplasmic vs extracellular pH) · release by BUDDING, which may give an envelope.
Cytopathic effectsSize/shape change · cytoplasmic and nuclear inclusion bodies · multinucleated fusion · lysis · DNA alteration ACTIVATING ONCOGENES · cancerous transformation.

Mutations & Control

TermWhat you need to know
MutationChange in the base sequence of the genome that may impact phenotype. Induced by radiation, chemicals, viruses. May be beneficial, neutral or deleterious.
3 routes of bacterial gene transferTRANSFORMATION (naked DNA from environment) · CONJUGATION (plasmid, cell to cell) · TRANSDUCTION (viral vector).
Viral mutation implicationsChanged surface properties may weaken/eliminate immunity from vaccine or prior exposure; animal viruses may cross to humans where no immunity exists.
Aseptic vs disinfectionAseptic/sterile = removal of ALL contamination. Disinfection = elimination of SOME.
2 control mechanismsAlteration of membrane permeability · denaturation of proteins and nucleic acids.
TemperatureHIGH can kill; LOW will NOT — only slows growth. Dry heat, wet heat (boiling, autoclave, pasteurization); refrigeration/freezing preserve.
Disinfectant vs antisepticDisinfectant on INANIMATE objects (Lysol). Antiseptic on TISSUES (Listerine).
True chemical sterilantsVery few: ethylene oxide (plastics, spices) · beta propiolactone (vaccines, tissue grafts, instruments).
Selective toxicityAntimicrobials should act on the microbe without harming the host. Sulfa drugs = synthetic (coal tar dyes); penicillin = from Penicillium, active against Gram-positives.
