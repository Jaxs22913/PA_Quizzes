PAJ 5310 · Physical Diagnosis II · Fall 2026

Cardiac OSCE — Original Rubrics, Combined

The five PD1 lab sheets this station draws on, word for word, on one page: General Survey (with the introduction and vital signs), Cardiovascular, Thorax & Lungs, Peripheral Vascular, and Skin, Hair & Nails. Nothing is reworded. A also … tag marks a step two sheets both grade: do it once, out loud. For the whole station in order see the run-sheet; for what each special maneuver finds, the special maneuvers guide.

Must be said out loud Performed — inspect / palpate / percuss / auscultate
Station — Wednesday 21 October 2026, 20–25 minutes. The chief complaint and vital signs are given on a separate form. You may bring diagnostic equipment and a pen, nothing else. The “patient” gives no verbal responses except identifying data — the facilitator answers for them, and you should always be looking at and interacting with the patient, not the facilitator. Faculty do not answer questions about the exam or technique once testing has started.

03 Vital Signs & Peripheral Pulses

Original: PAJ 5300 Introduction/General Survey/Vital Signs/Peripheral Pulses Lab Sheet (Summer 2026).

Height, Weight, BMI

  • 13Student verbalizes they would obtain height, weight, and calculate BMI

Body Temperature & Pulse Oximetry

  • 14Student verbalizes they would obtain a temperature & pulse ox.

Heart Rate

  • 15Locates the radial artery to check the heart rate (pulse)
  • 16Assesses pulse with the pads of the index and middle fingers
  • 17Student verbalizes the calculation they are using to assess the pulse (Must verbalize 30 seconds x2)

Respiratory Rate

  • 18Ensures adequate exposure of the chest
  • 19Maintains position as if counting pulse rate
  • 20Student verbalizes the calculation they are using to assess the respiratory rate

Blood Pressure

  • 21Positions patient properly – seated with back supported & feet on floor, legs uncrossed
  • 22Assesses for appropriate cuff size (Must verbalize)
  • 23Locates the brachial artery
  • 24Properly positions BP cuff (2.5 cm above antecubital fossa), artery line properly placed
  • 25Supports the arm with the brachial artery at or slightly above heart level
  • 26Determines the palpable radial pulse obliteration pressure (aka palpable systolic pressure and verbalizes reading (inflate cuff while palpating the radial artery until pulse no longer palpable)
  • 27Deflates cuff completely and waits 15-30 seconds
  • 28Then re-inflates cuff to 30mm Hg above palpable systolic pressure
  • 29Assesses for onset of Korotkoff sounds to determine systolic blood pressure
  • 30Deflates cuff slowly
  • 31Assesses for cessation of Korotkoff sounds to determine diastolic blood pressure
  • 32Repeat measurement on opposite arm
  • 33Student verbalizes if orthostatic measurement was indicated, they would also assess blood pressure supine and standing after waiting 1-3 minute between positions

Peripheral Pulses*

Palpates the following pulses bilaterally noting: rate, rhythm, and quality
  • 34also PVBrachial
  • 35also PVRadial
A complete peripheral vascular exam would also include assessment of the carotid, ulnar, femoral, popliteal, posterior tibialis, and dorsalis pedis pulses.

* The complete peripheral vascular exam is covered in detail during the Physical Examination of the Heart and Peripheral Vasculature lecture and lab. For the purposes of the vital signs, students must be able to locate the radial and brachial pulses.

04 Cardiovascular

Original: PAJ 5300 Cardiovascular Examination Lab Sheet (Summer 2026), items 1–26.

Preparation

  • 36Place the patient in the supine position for the cardiac examination with the head elevated 30 degrees
  • 37Stand on the right side if right-handed, or left side if left-handed of the patient during the examination.
  • 38also IntroPerform hand hygiene and ensure proper lighting.
  • 39also IntroIntroduce yourself. Obtain appropriate history questions and interpret vitals.
  • 40Visualize/identify carotid and jugular pulses.

Inspection

Indication: Inspect the precordium for abnormal wall motion or apical impulse
  • 41Inspect the precordium for abnormal wall motion and apical impulse using tangential lighting.

Carotid Pulse

Indication: to check the carotid pulse including the carotid upstroke, it’s amplitude and contour, and the presence of thrills.
  • 42Inspect the carotid pulses in the neck.
  • 43Ask the patient to hold their breath for ten seconds. Auscultating the carotids with the diaphragm and bell.
  • 44With the head of the bed still at 30 degrees, palpate each carotid artery in the lower 1/3 of the neck using your index and middle finger. Check bilaterally, but only palpate one carotid at a time.

Palpation of the precordium heaves and lifts using finger pads

Indication: palpate for various indicators such as heaves, palpable S1, S2, S3, or S4, the apical impulse and PMI.
  • 452nd intercostal space, right sternal border - Aortic Area
  • 462nd intercostal space, left sternal border - Pulmonic Area
  • 473rd intercostal space, left sternal border - Erb’s Point
  • 484th intercostal space, left sternal border - Tricuspid Area
  • 495th intercostal space, left mid-clavicular line - Mitral Area
  • 50Note the size and intensity of the apical impulse by palpation. Use palmar surface of several fingers. The apical impulse should be described as a light tap.
  • 51Note the location of the point of maximal impulse (PMI)

Palpation of the precordium for thrills, using the ball of your hand

Indication: palpate for various indicators such as a thrill, palpable S1, S2, S3, or S4, the apical impulse and PMI.
  • 522nd intercostal space, right sternal border - Aortic Area
  • 532nd intercostal space, left sternal border - Pulmonic Area
  • 543rd intercostal space, left sternal border - Erb’s Point
  • 554th intercostal space, left sternal border - Tricuspid Area
  • 565th intercostal space, left mid-clavicular line - Mitral Area

Auscultation of the precordium with the Diaphragm and Bell

Indication: rate, rhythm, intensities of S1/S2, systole, diastole, any extra sounds, any murmurs
  • 572nd intercostal space, right sternal border - Aortic Area
  • 582nd intercostal space, left sternal border - Pulmonic Area
  • 593rd intercostal space, left sternal border - Erb’s Point
  • 604th intercostal space, left sternal border - Tricuspid Area
  • 615th intercostal space, left mid-clavicular line - Mitral Area

05 Thorax & Lungs (Respiratory)

Original: PAJ 5300 Lungs and Thorax Lab Handout (Summer 2026).

Inspection

  • 62Inspects for the shape and movement of the thorax and any unilateral lag in movement.
  • 63Inspects for rate, rhythm, and effort of respiration.
  • 64Inspects for deformities, asymmetry, abnormal retractions, lesions, and any areas of ecchymosis.
  • 65Inspects for use of accessory muscles.
  • 66Inspects lips for cyanosis.
  • 67also PV · SkinInspects bilateral nail beds for cyanosis and clubbing.
  • 68Inspects anterior-posterior (AP) diameter.

Palpation

  • 69Palpates posterior chest wall for tenderness or deformities
  • 70Palpates lateral chest wall for tenderness or deformities
  • 71Palpates anterior chest wall for tenderness or deformities
  • 72Palpates thoracic expansion for symmetry of respiratory effort - POSTERIORLY
  • 73Palpates tactile fremitus bilaterally – POSTERIORLY, LATERALLY, and ANTERIORLY

Percussion

  • 74Percusses POSTERIOR chest wall, alternating sides.
  • 75Percusses LATERAL chest wall, alternating sides.
  • 76Percusses upper lobes ANTERIOR chest wall, alternating sides.
  • 77Measures diaphragmatic excursion - in centimeters (4 - 6 cm)Estimates the diaphragmatic excursion by determining the distance between the level of dullness on full expiration and the level of dullness on full inspiration

Auscultation

  • 78Asks patient to breathe through mouth during auscultation
  • 79Asks the patient to cross arms for posterior examination
  • 80Verbalizes and demonstrates performing one full cycle of inhalation and expiration at each point.
  • 81Auscultates POSTERIOR chest wall, alternating sides bilaterally in a ladder pattern
  • 82Auscultates LATERAL chest wall, alternating sides bilaterally in a ladder pattern
  • 83Auscultates upper lobes ANTERIOR chest wall, alternating sides bilaterally in a ladder pattern
  • 84Verbalizes if breath sounds appropriate for area of auscultation (vesicular, bronchovesicular, bronchial, tracheal)
  • 85Verbalizes listening for crackles, rhonchi, wheezes, pleural rubs
  • 86Verbalizes if adventitious sounds are present, student states, “I will perform one of three tests, egophony, bronchophony, and /or whispered pectoriloquy.”

07 Skin, Hair & Nails

Original: PAJ 5300 Skin, Hair, and Nails Lab Sheet (Summer 2026).

• Skin should be inspected for: Color, uniformity, thickness, symmetry, hygiene, lesions, rashes, dryness, odors
  o Inspection of body hair distribution may be performed with skin inspection
• Skin should be palpated for: Moisture, temperature, texture, turgor, mobility
• Nails should be inspected for: Color, condition, cleanliness, length, lesions, redness/swelling of nail folds, nail base angle
• Nails should be palpated for: Texture, firmness, thickness, uniformity, adherence to nail bed
• Hair should be inspected for: Color, distribution, quantity
• Hair Should be palpated for: Texture
