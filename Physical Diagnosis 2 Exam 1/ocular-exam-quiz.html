Before you begin

Thirty questions on the advanced ocular history and examination. Covers the four-part symptom history, the acute-versus-gradual and painful-versus-painless patterns, the common mistakes, the suggested order of the examination, inspection of the lids and conjunctiva, visual acuity and the pinhole test, confrontation fields, ocular motility, the pupils and the swinging light test, fundoscopy, ocular trauma, and referral timing. Built from the 26 August recording, not the deck alone. Professor Beck removes slides from scope out loud, and the deck cannot say which ones — so this was held until the audio existed. Six things she excluded are absent here, each because she said so: the named virus in viral conjunctivitis, the exophthalmometer technique and its measurement, the strabismus diagram (“just a visual … you don’t have to memorize”), the corneal reflection test (“we already did that in PD1”), the Adie’s pupil look-alike associations (“it’s not on my test this time”), and the Latin expansions of OD, OS and OU. Adie’s pupil itself is in — “you should know Adie’s pupil, that could be on my test” — and so are the abbreviations themselves, which she called “terms that you must remember”. The red-eye comparison chart gets its own questions, because she singled it out: “I genuinely think it’s important that you are very familiar with that chart.” That slide extracts as completely blank — it is a picture of the table — so its content was recovered by optical character recognition and is marked image-only in the citations. Every question cites its slide, or the recording.
