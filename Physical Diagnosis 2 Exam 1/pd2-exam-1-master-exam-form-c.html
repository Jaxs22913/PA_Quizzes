Before you begin

Sixty questions drawn from every lecture in the Exam 1 block, in proportion rather than at random. Dermatology, ocular and ENT contribute nineteen questions each; clinical reasoning contributes three — that lecture is a 24-page introduction, much of it course mechanics, which the house rule keeps out of questions, so three is an honest share rather than a shortfall. No question appears in more than one form, so working through all five gives you 300 distinct questions. These are drawn from the full authored pools, not just the two topic quizzes per lecture — over half the written material never reaches a topic quiz, and this is where it goes. This is a physical diagnosis course, so the questions ask what you find and what finding it means; the mechanism behind these conditions belongs to Clinical Pathophysiology and their management to Clinical Medicine and Surgery. The tuning fork block carries extra weight throughout, because the class was told it is worth three points on test day. Every question cites its slide.
