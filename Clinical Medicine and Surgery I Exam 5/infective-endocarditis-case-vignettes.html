Written in the shape of Professor Reynolds' own practice cases for this lecture: a short case with the age, the risk history, vital signs, the findings described rather than named, and the culture or echocardiogram result, then one question: the recommended initial treatment, the most important next step, who needs prophylaxis, the modified Duke classification, the likely organism, the surgical indication, or what to use instead when a comorbidity or allergy rules out the usual drug.

Her four practice cases are included (two in each quiz), keyed as she keyed them; one of them had five choices and is shown here with four. Everything else is new. Four options, A–D. Each choice explains why it fits or fails this patient, and every question cites its slide.
