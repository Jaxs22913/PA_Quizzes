What's new

August 18
  • Principles of Diagnostic Medicine I has started. Lecture 1, Principles of Laboratory Diagnostics — 2 quizzes (60 questions) covering the pretest, intratest and posttest phases, the order of draw and which tube goes with which test, stool, blood, sputum and throat studies, point-of-care testing with its regulation, and sensitivity, specificity, predictive values and pre-test probability. Built to match how Professor Reynolds said this course is examined: reference ranges are always supplied, so nothing here requires a memorised normal value, and no question asks you to calculate a predictive value — she said outright she will not make you do math. Tube questions stay on the order of draw and the common colour-to-test pairings rather than the full additive table, which is also what she asked for.
  • Physical Diagnosis 2 has started. Lecture 1, Introduction to Physical Diagnosis II, gets a single 15-question quiz rather than the usual two × 30 — most of that deck is course orientation, and questions about grade weightings and file naming are not worth your time. What is left is clinical: the oral case presentation, comprehensive versus focused encounters, and the documentation rules. Lecture 2, Dermatology, gets the full 2 × 30 (60 questions) covering the history, the examination itself, the descriptive vocabulary, and the abnormal findings of skin, hair and nails. Also: a new study guide with both lectures, a new Cram Sheet (6 topics, 47 rows), and 3 Arcade decks (110 cards). Exam 1 covers Lectures 1–4, so Ophthalmology and ENT sections get added as those decks are posted.
  • Three OSCE run-sheets for Physical Diagnosis 2 — ENT (16 September), Cardiac (21 October) and Pulmonary (10 November). These cover the whole station rather than just the maneuvers: introduction, focused history of present illness, focused review of systems and background history, vital sign interpretation, the system examination, then differentials, studies with justification, diagnosis, treatment plan with patient education, and the one-minute case presentation — the component list from the course lecture, in order. 362 items across the three, each tagged spoken or hands-on and carrying the exact words to say. Same instrument as the PD1 head-to-toe checksheet: ticks persist, spoken-only and hands-on-only filters, and it prints. Examination items come from the PD1 lab checksheets, since those maneuvers do not change between courses; whatever the PD2 lectures add gets layered in as each is posted.

Mechanism only: the same material is taught in Clinical Medicine and Surgery I this term, and that is where diagnosis and management live.

Lecture 1 — Antibiotics, Antivirals and Antifungals — covers 23 drug classes across 128 slides, so it is built as three topics: Antibacterials (2 × 30), Antifungals (2 × 30) and Antivirals (2 × 20) — 160 questions in all. Weighted toward class identity and mechanism of action, then black box warnings and high-yield adverse effects. Drug dosages are not tested, per Dr. Wood. The antiviral sets are twenty rather than thirty because that section is eleven slides covering six agents; padding to thirty would mean asking the same facts twice.
