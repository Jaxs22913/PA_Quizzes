How This Exam Is Written

TermWhat you need to know
Reference ranges are GIVEN“I'm not going to just throw a random number at you” — a rate, an interval or a potassium in a stem comes with the range that reads it.

Next-best-testOften another lead: posterior leads V7–V9 for isolated ST depression in V1–V4; V4R for an inferior infarction with hypotension.

TermWhat you need to know
★ The atrial rule“If there is P waves, the rhythm is atrial. Remember that, that's very important.”

Conduction System & Pacemakers

TermWhat you need to know
Sinoatrial nodeMain pacemaker, upper right atrial wall below the superior vena cava. 60–100 per minute.
Atrioventricular nodeNear the coronary sinus. BACKUP pacemaker, 40–60 per minute. Zones: transition, compact, trigger.
Purkinje networkThird pacemaker (both nodes failed), 20–40 per minute. Conducts FASTEST of all.
Bachmann's bundleRight atrium to left atrium: the main interatrial route.
Internodal tractsSuperior anterior (FAST), middle, inferior posterior (SLOW). In most people only the fast tract truly reaches the node.
Bundle of HisThe ONLY path atria → ventricles in a normal heart (the atrioventricular septum does not conduct).
Bundle branchesLeft splits into THREE fascicles: posterior, septal (septum = Q wave), anterior. Right is MUCH LONGER.
Passive filling70–80% of ventricular filling needs no atrial contraction.

Cardiac Cell & Action Potential

TermWhat you need to know
Four propertiesAUTOMATICITY (own impulse) · EXCITABILITY (responds) · CONTRACTILITY · CONDUCTIVITY (cell to cell). The pacemaker hierarchy keeps lower cells quiet.
Phase order4 → 0 → 1 → 2 → 3.
Phase 4 / 04 = rest, −90 millivolts, potassium inside. 0 = upstroke: fast SODIUM rushes in.
Phase 1 / 2 / 31 = the notch. 2 = plateau: CALCIUM in → contraction. 3 = repolarization: calcium closes, POTASSIUM OUT, back to −90 (slides say potassium “in”; truth and the slide 17 figure say out).
Absolute refractoryNo response at all; about 180 milliseconds; QRS start to the T wave PEAK.
Relative refractoryResponds, but fragile; T wave peak to end. The T wave peak divides the two.
VectorLeft and down, toward the front. Lead II looks straight along it: the rhythm lead.

Paper, Waves & Intervals

TermWhat you need to know
Paper25 millimeters per second. Small box 0.04 SECONDS, 1 millimeter (slide says “0.04ms”). Large box 0.2 seconds, 5 millimeters. Horizontal = time, vertical = voltage. Calibration 10 millimeters tall.
P waveAtrial depolarization. Round, upright in lead II, ≤2.5 millimeters, under 0.12 seconds.
★ PR intervalP onset to QRS start. 0.12–0.20 seconds (3–5 small boxes), constant. Blocks, accessory pathways, alternate pacemakers move it.
★ QRSVentricular depolarization; a complex of Q, R, S. Under 0.12 seconds; 0.12 or more = abnormal ventricular conduction.
Q / R / SQ = septum (away from lead II), under 0.04 seconds. R = ventricular depolarization toward lead II (deck: anterior left ventricle). S = away from lead II (deck: lateral wall); must cross baseline, else “S wave pattern”.
J point / ST / TJ = QRS meets ST, at baseline ±1 millimeter. ST = ventricles holding contraction. T = ventricular repolarization, asymmetric.
QTQRS start to T end; all ventricular activity. 350–450 milliseconds males, 360–460 females. QTc: Lecture 7 (no slide here).
TP segmentResting state; the BEST isoelectric reference (not the PR segment).

Leads

TermWhat you need to know
Bipolar I, II, IIIEinthoven's triangle. Lead II: POSITIVE LEFT FOOT, negative right shoulder. Lead I: left shoulder +, right shoulder −. Lead III: left foot +, left shoulder −.
Unipolar augmentedaVR, aVL, aVF (augmented vector right, left, foot): one physical lead + a theoretical central terminal (the deck says Wilson's; strictly that is the chest leads' reference).
Chest leadsUnipolar, HORIZONTAL view. V1 4th space right of sternum · V2 4th left · V3 between · V4 5th midclavicular · V5 anterior axillary · V6 midaxillary.
Who looks whereV1–V2 septal · V3–V4 anterior · I, aVL, V5, V6 lateral · II, III, aVF inferior. Contiguous = same area.

Rate, Regularity & the Five Steps

TermWhat you need to know
★ Six-second methodQRS count × 10 = ventricular rate; P waves × 10 = atrial rate. The most used; works on irregular rhythms.
Count-downHeavy lines from an R wave: 300, 150, 100, 75, 60, 50. Unreliable below 50.
300 / 1500300 ÷ large boxes; 1500 ÷ small boxes between R waves. 1500 is the MOST ACCURATE.
RegularityCompare consecutive R to R intervals; calipers (or marked paper) if unsure.
★ Five stepsRate → regularity → P waves → PR interval → QRS. Every strip, every time.
Married P wavesEach P wave is causing its QRS. A P before every QRS = atrial origin; uniform upright P = sinus node.

The Sinus Rhythms

TermWhat you need to know
Normal sinus rhythm60–100, regular, normal married P waves, PR 0.12–0.20 constant, narrow QRS.
Bradycardia / tachycardiaSame criteria; rate under 60 / over 100. QRS narrow or wide. RATE is the only difference.
Sinus arrhythmia60–100, IRREGULAR, otherwise sinus. Benign, most common in children. Inspiration → vagal tone suppressed → rate UP; exhalation → down.
Sinus exit blockNode fires, impulse BLOCKED: whole cycle missing (no P, QRS, T). P to P MARCHES OUT (gap = multiple of the cycle).
Sinus pauseNode STOPS and restarts; cycle RESETS; does NOT march out.
Sinus arrestLike a pause but AT LEAST THREE cycles missed; resets.
Sick sinus syndromeExit block + pause + arrest in one patient (even one strip). Rare.

Lecture 12: How It Is Asked

TermWhat you need to know
★ Said out loudSawtooth = flutter (“the only rhythm that will have this pattern”); irregularly irregular = fibrillation; no P + narrow + regular + over 150 = supraventricular tachycardia.
Not nowNo treatment (“solely about rhythm interpretation and diagnosis”). Fibrillation types: “don't get too much into the weeds”.

Premature Beats

TermWhat you need to know
Premature atrial complexEARLY, from the atria outside the sinus node (increased automaticity); P wave of NEW SHAPE; narrow QRS.
Premature junctional complexEARLY, narrow; P wave NONE, INVERTED, or AFTER the QRS. Isolated ones: no clinical significance.
Premature ventricular complexEARLY, WIDE (typically over 0.12 s), NO P wave, T wave OPPOSITE the QRS.
GroupingsCouplet = 2 · triplet = 3. Same shape = UNIFOCAL (one focus); different shapes = MULTIFOCAL.
Patterns (all three types)Every other beat = BIGEMINY · every 3rd = TRIGEMINY · every 4th = QUADRIGEMINY.
★ R on TA premature ventricular complex landing on the prior T wave (relative refractory period) can trigger ventricular tachycardia or fibrillation.
OutputFrequent or grouped premature ventricular complexes can reduce cardiac output.
Run of ventricular tachycardiaSlide 70 says 6 in a row; the usual definition is 3 or more consecutive ventricular beats. A brief (nonsustained) run needs evaluation.

Escape Beats & Rhythms

TermWhat you need to know
Escape = LATEArrives past the expected R wave, after a pause, because the pacemaker above failed or slowed.
Junctional escape beatSinus node fails or slows; late narrow beat; the sinus node then resets.
Junctional escape rhythmEscape beats become the rhythm: the atrioventricular node takes over and suppresses the sinus node.
Ventricular escape complexLate and WIDE: BOTH nodes failed.
Atrial escapeNamed in the objective; no slide in the deck.

Junctional Rhythms

TermWhat you need to know
P wave ruleAtria first → INVERTED P just before the QRS (short PR). Together → NO P. Ventricles first (trigger zone) → P AFTER the QRS.
Junctional escape40–60 per minute.
Accelerated junctional60–100 per minute.
Junctional tachycardiaOver 100 per minute.
All threeREGULAR (an irregular spot = a premature junctional complex); PR under 0.12 s if a P precedes; QRS usually narrow.
Node zonesTRANSITION = arrhythmogenic, most premature junctional complexes · COMPACT = slows conduction, backup pacemaker · TRIGGER = fires down the His bundle.

Idioventricular Rhythms

TermWhat you need to know
Idioventricular rhythmPurkinje network pacing. 20–40 per minute, REGULAR, WIDE (over 0.12 s), NO P waves.
Accelerated idioventricularSame, 40–100 per minute.
Versus junctionalJunctional: 40–60, P absent or inverted, usually NARROW. Idioventricular: 20–40, no P, WIDE. Both regular.
“Agonal rhythm”Slide 75 equates it with idioventricular rhythm; agonal is the very slow (under about 20) dying end.

Supraventricular Dysrhythmias

TermWhat you need to know
Wandering atrial pacemakerAt least 3 P wave shapes, married; IRREGULAR; under 100; PR variable.
Multifocal atrial tachycardiaThe same, OVER 100 per minute (the deck's atrial tachycardia).
★ Atrial flutterReentry circuit, usually RIGHT atrium (enlarged, scarred, ischemic tissue). SAWTOOTH = pathognomonic. Atrial rate about 300.
Flutter ratioDocument flutter waves per QRS: 2:1, 3:1. Ratio changing = VARIABLE CONDUCTION (irregular). Count troughs to the next QRS; a notch on the QRS counts.
★ Atrial fibrillationMany atrial sites, sinus node suppressed, atrial KICK lost. No P waves, fibrillatory waves, IRREGULARLY IRREGULAR, atrial rate indeterminate.
★ Supraventricular tachycardiaAtrioventricular nodal reentry: REGULAR, NARROW, 150–300, P NOT VISIBLE (or retrograde), started by a premature atrial complex.
Typical vs atypicalTypical: DOWN SLOW, UP FAST; no P or pseudo S waves. Atypical: down fast, up slow; retrograde P clearly visible (RP interval).
Rate ≠ rhythmUpright sinus P before every QRS at 190 is still SINUS TACHYCARDIA. Unsure P or T? Call it a T wave.

Ventricular Rhythms (Lecture 13)

TermWhat you need to know
Ventricular tachycardiaANY tachycardia that originates in the ventricles. 100–300 per minute, REGULAR (monomorphic), no P waves (or DISSOCIATED ones), no PR, WIDE QRS. With OR without a pulse.
Polymorphic / torsades de pointesIRREGULAR ventricular tachycardia, 100–300, complexes TWIST around the baseline ("twisting of the points"). ★ Usually a consequence of a PROLONGED QT interval.
Ventricular fibrillationChaotic firing of MULTIPLE ventricular foci; the muscle QUIVERS. 300–500, irregular, NO measurable P, QRS or T. No contraction = NO cardiac output, NO pulse. Fine (small waves) or coarse.
AsystoleLack of electrical activity, no pulse: FLAT LINE. Untreated ventricular fibrillation ends here.
Pulseless electrical activityORGANIZED electrical activity, NO mechanical activity. ★ Can look like ANY organized rhythm, even normal sinus; the missing PULSE makes the diagnosis.
Ventricular standstillAtria contract, ventricles do not: upright P waves MARCH across with NO QRS. No pulse, no cardiac output. Can be paroxysmal.

Telling the Ventricular Rhythms Apart

TermWhat you need to know
Monomorphic vs polymorphicONE shape, regular vs CHANGING shape, irregular. Rate and width are shared and cannot separate them.
Tachycardia vs fibrillationORGANIZED identical complexes vs CHAOS with no measurable QRS. "You can see how organized V-tach truly is."
Fine fibrillation vs asystoleSmall continuous waves vs a FLAT line.
Standstill vs asystoleStandstill keeps its P waves; asystole has none.

Paced Rhythms

TermWhat you need to know
Atrial pacingSpike → NORMAL P-QRS-T. Depends on an INTACT conduction pathway, so the QRS is narrow.
Ventricular pacing★ Spike married to EVERY QRS + WIDE QRS + CONTRALATERAL T wave (opposite to the QRS) = ventricle captured. Paces below the atria.
Both chambersRare: a spike before the P wave AND before each wide QRS.
Wide rhythm?Always look for pacer spikes.

Atrioventricular Blocks

TermWhat you need to know
What a block isA DELAY in, or complete FAILURE of, conduction of an atrial impulse through the atrioventricular node.
First-degree★ Sinus rhythm with PR BEYOND 0.2 s; ALL P waves conducted; BENIGN delay in the atrioventricular node. "The only thing you need to know … is the PR interval duration."
Second-degree type I (Wenckebach, Mobitz I)PR LENGTHENS until a P wave is not conducted, then resets: grouped beats. Irregular. In the atrioventricular NODE: conduction slows beat by beat until one impulse fails.
Second-degree type II (Mobitz II)CONSTANT PR (normal or wide) with dropped QRS; ≥2 consecutive conducted beats before a drop; can drop >1 in a row. LESS common, MORE serious, ALWAYS conduction disease. BELOW the node (His-Purkinje). QRS usually wide.
Third-degree (complete)No P waves conducted: regular P-P UNRELATED to regular R-R, P waves march through the QRS-T. Atrial rate FASTER. Two pacemakers. QRS narrow or wide by escape origin.
Third-degree causesAge-related FIBROSIS and acute myocardial infarction, especially INFERIOR.

Block Logic

TermWhat you need to know
★ Cheat code (type I vs II)Find the dropped beat. Compare PR BEFORE vs AFTER it: DIFFERENT = type I (after is shorter); EQUAL = type II.
His two questionsMore P waves than QRS? → a block. Then PR lengthening = type I, constant = type II, random = third-degree. (Long PR, nothing dropped = first-degree.)
Escape in complete blockAtrioventricular junction → NARROW QRS. Purkinje / bundle-branch fibers → WIDE QRS.
Ventricular tachycardia P wavesThe ventricular impulse does not go back up to the atria, so any P waves are dissociated.

Axis (Lecture 14)

TermWhat you need to know
Normal-30 to +90 degrees: lead I UP, aVF UP.
Left axis deviationBeyond -30: lead I UP, aVF DOWN. Causes include left anterior fascicular block, past inferior infarction, ventricular pacing.
Right axis deviationBeyond +90: lead I DOWN, aVF UP. Causes include chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, pulmonary embolism, dextrocardia, left posterior fascicular block; NORMAL IN CHILDREN.
Extreme (no man's land)-90 to 180: lead I DOWN, aVF DOWN. Causes include ventricular arrhythmia, hyperkalemia, lead transposition. ★ Wide tachycardia + extreme axis → think VENTRICULAR TACHYCARDIA.
Quick methodLead I and aVF; is each QRS mostly positive or mostly negative?
Slide errorsSlide 22's right-axis quadrant is drawn reversed (I down, aVF up is correct). Slide 21 lists left ventricular hypertrophy under right axis; it causes LEFT axis deviation.

Bundle Branch Blocks

TermWhat you need to know
BothQRS 0.12 s or LONGER (3 small boxes). V1 decides the side.
★ J point rule (V1)Travel BACK from the J point: first deflection NEGATIVE = LEFT; POSITIVE = RIGHT. (Left blinker goes down.)
Left bundle branch blockrS in V1; NOTCHED lateral QRS (I, aVL, V5, V6; double QRS best in V6); NO lateral Q waves.
Right bundle branch blockrSR in V1; SLURRED S waves in I, aVL, V5, V6. Bunny ears unreliable: many V1 shapes, but always ends positive.
Why wideBlocked side waits for slow MYOCYTE-TO-MYOCYTE spread; ventricles depolarize in SEQUENCE (two-QRS phenomenon).
Left block physiologyRight ventricle first; septal fascicle blocked → septal Q waves lost laterally.
Incomplete blockNot in the deck or recording; not quizzed.

Fascicular Blocks

TermWhat you need to know
Left bundleSplits into ANTERIOR, POSTERIOR and SEPTAL fascicles.
Left anterior (hemiblock)LEFT axis, q in I and r in III (q1r3), II and III negative. ★ MOST COMMON intraventricular conduction abnormality in ACUTE MYOCARDIAL INFARCTION.
Left posterior (hemiblock)RIGHT axis, r in I and q in III (r1q3), III positive; lead II not significant. FAR LESS common.

Atrial Enlargement

TermWhat you need to know
Enlargement meansSTRETCHING that DILATES a chamber; most seen in the ATRIA (valve disease, pulmonary hypertension, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, pulmonary embolism).
Right (P PULMONALE)Limb leads: upright P TALLER than 2.5 mm. V1: biphasic, LARGER POSITIVE first part.
Left (P MITRALE)Limb leads: HUMPED P ≥0.12 s, humps ≥0.04 s apart. V1: biphasic, LARGE NEGATIVE terminal part.
One lead is enoughEither criterion in ONE lead confirms it.

Ventricular Hypertrophy

TermWhat you need to know
Hypertrophy meansTHICKENING of the muscle that makes the chamber SMALLER.
Right ventricular hypertrophyMORE R than S in V1 and V2. Supported by right atrial enlargement, right axis deviation, STRAIN. Excluded by RIGHT BUNDLE BRANCH BLOCK, posterior infarction, age under 7 or 8.
Strain patternST from the J point into an INVERTED, ASYMMETRICAL T wave, V1 and V2.
Left ventricular hypertrophyLEFT axis; DEEP S in V1 and V2, TALL R in I, aVL, V5, V6. Any ONE: S (V1/V2) + R (V5/V6) ≥35 mm; R in aVL >12 mm; any chest lead >45 mm.
★ Infarction mimicLeft ventricular hypertrophy is the MOST COMMON: CONCAVE upward ST elevation V1–V3 + lateral T inversion. Others: pericarditis, early repolarization, Brugada, left bundle branch block and ventricular pacing, hypothermia.

The Systematic 12-Lead

TermWhat you need to know
OrderGeneral impression → calibration → AXIS → QRS (wide? blocks?) → hypertrophy/enlargement → ST and T changes.
Standard calibrationONE big box wide, TWO big boxes tall.
Name the rhythm firstA bundle branch block is NOT a rhythm: "sinus tachycardia with a left bundle branch block."

Ischemia, Injury, Infarction: the Ladder

TermWhat you need to know
Hyperacute T wavesOften the VERY FIRST sign of ischemia. Broad, tall, SYMMETRICAL (big enough to hold the QRS). Normal T = asymmetrical, slow upstroke, sharper fall. Easily missed: no ST change to call yet.
ST depressionSUBENDOCARDIAL ischemia (inner layer). Often GLOBAL, in nearly every lead.
ST elevationTRANSMURAL ischemia = INJURY (full wall thickness).
Pathologic Q wavesINFARCTION: death of tissue, from a previous OR an acute event. Longer than 0.04 s (one small box) AND deeper than 2 mm, or more than 25% of the R wave. No R wave = QS complex, still pathologic in an infarction.
T-wave inversionNo slide. In lecture: a sign of ischemia, LATER than hyperacute T waves.

J Point & Baseline

TermWhat you need to know
J pointJunction of the QRS and the ST segment. ST segment = J point to onset of the T wave. Healthy heart: both AT BASELINE.
Ischemia moves itAcute coronary syndrome can push the J point ABOVE (ST elevation) or BELOW (ST depression) the baseline.
BaselineCompare the J point with the TP SEGMENT. Fast rate, no TP segment: compare with the ONSET OF THE QRS.

Coronary Arteries

TermWhat you need to know
Left coronary (left main)Divides into the CIRCUMFLEX and the LEFT ANTERIOR DESCENDING.
Right coronaryRight atrium, RIGHT VENTRICLE, BOTTOM (inferior) of the left ventricle, back of the septum. ★ Inferior wall = right coronary (about 80-85% of people, said in lecture).
Left anterior descendingFRONT (and bottom) of the left ventricle, FRONT of the septum → septal, anterior, anteroseptal infarction.
CircumflexLeft atrium, SIDE and BACK of the left ventricle → lateral, high lateral, POSTERIOR infarction.
Coronary veinsReturn used, oxygen-poor blood to the right atrium.

Leads → Wall → Reciprocal

TermWhat you need to know
SeptalV1, V2 · reciprocal NONE
AnteriorV3, V4 · reciprocal NONE
AnteroseptalV1-V4 · reciprocal NONE (left anterior descending)
LateralI, aVL, V5, V6 · reciprocal II, III, aVF
AnterolateralI, aVL, V3-V6 · reciprocal II, III, aVF
InferiorII, III, aVF · reciprocal I, aVL (right coronary)
High lateralI, aVL · reciprocal III, aVF (circumflex in lecture; a diagonal of the left anterior descending can also supply it)
PosteriorNO facing lead · reciprocal V1-V4 (circumflex)
aVRThe only lead with no wall assigned.
★ Most common reciprocal pairLead III and lead aVL (in lecture).

ST-Elevation Infarction & Reciprocal Changes

TermWhat you need to know
Criterion1 mm or more in 2 or more CONTIGUOUS leads, with or without reciprocal changes. (V2-V3 thresholds are higher, given in lecture: women 1.5 mm, men 40 and over 2 mm, men under 40 2.5 mm.)
Reciprocal changesMirror image on the OPPOSITE leads. ST depression with ST elevation on the same tracing = ALWAYS reciprocal.
★ Confirm, never rule outReciprocal changes CONFIRM ischemia when ST elevation is present; their ABSENCE does NOT exclude it.

Posterior Leads & V4R

TermWhat you need to know
★ Posterior triggerISOLATED ST depression in V1-V4 with NO elevation anywhere = reciprocal of a POSTERIOR infarction until proven otherwise. Obtain posterior leads.
Posterior tracingMove V4, V5, V6 to the BACK as V7, V8, V9 (the 15-lead). ST ELEVATION there confirms. 3-4% of infarctions; LEFT CIRCUMFLEX.
★ Right ventricleInferior infarction (RIGHT CORONARY) + HYPOTENSION, CLEAR LUNGS, JUGULAR VEIN DISTENSION → suspect right ventricular involvement.
V4RV4 to the RIGHT 5th intercostal space, midclavicular line. ELEVATION in V4R confirms right ventricular involvement.
Why it matters (lecture)Right ventricular infarction makes output preload-dependent; nitroglycerin can cause collapse. More elevation in III than II raises suspicion before V4R.

Pericarditis vs Early Repolarization vs Infarction

TermWhat you need to know
InfarctionLOCALIZED to contiguous leads, convex, reciprocal depression, Q waves.
PericarditisInflamed space between visceral and parietal pericardium. PR DEPRESSION, PR ELEVATION in aVR, GLOBAL CONCAVE ST elevation, NO reciprocal depression except aVR and V1.
Not pericarditis ifST depression in ANY lead other than aVR and V1, or ANY Q wave → consider an acute infarction.
Benign early repolarizationNormal variant ST elevation. Mostly YOUNG MEN. NO clinical significance. Global concave elevation, J-point notch (J wave, fishhook), large T waves, no reciprocal depression, no PR depression.
Lead V6 ratioST height ÷ T amplitude in V6: OVER 25% = pericarditis; UNDER 25% = early repolarization (more T wave than ST).

Brugada Syndrome

TermWhat you need to know
WhatInherited arrhythmogenic disease of the SODIUM channels of the RIGHT VENTRICULAR OUTFLOW TRACT. Young men of SOUTHEAST ASIAN descent.
Type 1Convex, COVED ST elevation.
Type 2SADDLEBACK ST elevation.
★ LeadsBoth types isolated to V1-V3 ("for a test question").

Bundle Branch Block, Pacing & Sgarbossa

TermWhat you need to know
ProblemLeft bundle branch block and ventricular pacing: WIDE QRS, GLOBAL ST elevation and T-wave changes → an infarction is hard to locate.
Sgarbossa (any one)CONCORDANT elevation 1 mm or more (positive QRS) · CONCORDANT depression 1 mm or more in V1-V3 · DISCORDANT elevation 5 mm or more (negative QRS).
Paced rhythmPacing SPIKE (artifact) before each WIDE QRS, contralateral (discordant) T waves, global ST elevation.

Preexcitation & Reentry

TermWhat you need to know
PreexcitationVentricles activated EARLY through an accessory pathway that BYPASSES the atrioventricular node. Short PR + delta wave. Most common: WOLFF-PARKINSON-WHITE.
Wolff-Parkinson-WhitePathway = bundle of KENT. Triad: SHORT PR, WIDE QRS, DELTA WAVE (slurred QRS upstroke).
Type A / Type BA = LEFT-sided Kent, QRS mostly POSITIVE in V1. B = RIGHT-sided, mostly NEGATIVE in V1.
OrthodromicDown the NORMAL pathway, up the accessory → regular NARROW tachycardia.
AntidromicDown the ACCESSORY pathway, up the normal → regular, monomorphic, WIDE tachycardia.
Can't tellMay be impossible to tell from supraventricular or ventricular tachycardia (treat a wide one as ventricular tachycardia). Unknown pathway: once cardioversion restores sinus rhythm, look for delta waves.

Potassium & Calcium

TermWhat you need to know
HyperkalemiaPEAKED symmetric T (early) → FLAT P → WIDE QRS → SINE WAVE. Can also cause bradycardias, tachycardias, ST elevation, blocks.
HypokalemiaBelow 3.5 mEq/L. Prolongs repolarization: T FLATTENING, PROMINENT U WAVES, T-U fusion, prominent P, ST depression (ST elevation in aVR).
HypocalcemiaPhase 2 PROLONGED: LONG ST, NORMAL T, prolonged QTc.
HypercalcemiaPhase 2 SHORTENED: SHORT ST, shortened QTc, T wave against the QRS.
SodiumNo tracing or rhythm change (slide 46).

Hypothermia, Pulmonary Embolism, Thyroid

TermWhat you need to know
HypothermiaNear 35°C (95°F): SINUS BRADYCARDIA, then long intervals. Below 32°C (90°F): OSBORN waves (terminal QRS notch), often mistaken for an infarction.
Pulmonary embolismTACHYCARDIA (hypoxia → sympathetic), RIGHTWARD axis (right ventricular dilation), ST-T changes (localized ischemia), S1Q3T3 (deep S in I, Q in III, inverted T in III).
HyperthyroidismSINUS TACHYCARDIA most common; atrial fibrillation second.
HypothyroidismSinus BRADYCARDIA, global LOW VOLTAGE, flat or inverted T, prolonged QTc, low P waves.
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and long QT syndromeSyllabus objectives with no slide; QT prolongation is listed under calcium, thyroid and drugs.

Drug Toxicities

TermWhat you need to know
DigoxinBuildup OR an overly sensitive heart. Inhibits the SODIUM-POTASSIUM PUMP. FREQUENT PREMATURE VENTRICULAR COMPLEXES most common (bigeminy). (Slide says intracellular potassium rises; truth: sodium and calcium rise inside, potassium stays outside.)
AmiodaroneBlocks sodium, potassium and calcium channels + BETA BLOCKER. Slows conduction, SLOWS PHASE 3 → QT PROLONGATION, suppresses the sinoatrial node, slows the atrioventricular node.
Tricyclic antidepressantFast SODIUM-channel blockade (widens the QRS, as on the slide's tracing) with delayed potassium outflow → QT PROLONGATION; ANTICHOLINERGIC → tachycardia.
