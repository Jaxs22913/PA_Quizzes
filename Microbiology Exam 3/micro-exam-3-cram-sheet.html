L14 · Endotoxin & Sorting

TermWhat you need to know
EndotoxinLIPOPOLYSACCHARIDE of the outer membrane. LIPID A = biologically active part. Released on LYSIS (including by antibiotics) and during rapid growth.
Three partsLipid A (active) · core polysaccharide (same for large groups) · O antigen (unique per organism; repeat number = rough, semi-rough, smooth).
Usually protectiveInnate immunity via a lipopolysaccharide binding complex + Toll-like receptor → moderated inflammation keeps mucosal infection mucosal.
DamagingComplement (hemorrhagic necrosis) · coagulation (disseminated intravascular coagulation, bradykinin) · high levels impair neutrophil CHEMOTAXIS · macrophages release tumor necrosis factor, interleukins 1 and 6.
Low vs floodLow: fever, malaise, aches. Flood: SEPTIC SHOCK — microthrombi, vasodilation, cardiac output first UP (warm shock), then down → organ failure.
Three sorting propertiesOXYGEN requirement · LACTOSE fermentation · OXIDASE enzyme.
Aerobes, no fermentationPseudomonas, Burkholderia, Bordetella, Francisella, Brucella, Alcaligenes. Pairs: Pseudomonas + Burkholderia OPPORTUNISTS · Brucella + Francisella ZOONOTIC · Bordetella + Legionella HUMAN.
Facultative anaerobesLactose + (coliforms): Escherichia, Klebsiella, Citrobacter, Enterobacter. Lactose −, oxidase −: Salmonella, Shigella, Proteus, Yersinia and others. Lactose −, OXIDASE +: Haemophilus, Pasteurella. Obligate anaerobe: Bacteroides.

L14 · Pseudomonas & Its Relatives

TermWhat you need to know
PseudomonadsSmall rods, ONE POLAR flagellum · aerobic, NO fermentation · oxidase + catalase + · water-soluble pigments · soil and water.
P. aeruginosa signsGRAPELIKE odor · greenish-blue pigment PYOCYANIN · resists soaps, quaternary ammonium disinfectants, drying.
Why hospitalsBIOFILMS → contaminates ventilators, intravenous solutions, anesthesia equipment. Major nosocomial agent.
VirulenceExotoxin A blocks PROTEIN SYNTHESIS · slime layer (anti-phagocytosis, biofilm) · exoenzyme S → T cell APOPTOSIS · multidrug resistant (permeability, enzymes, efflux pumps, gene transfer).
Disease★ Burns, neoplastic disease, CYSTIC FIBROSIS. Septicemia → endocarditis, meningitis. Healthy people: rash, urinary tract infection, SWIMMER'S EAR, CORNEAL ULCER (contact lenses).
VaccineIn development, for cystic fibrosis; but in CHRONIC cystic fibrosis infection it may worsen lung inflammation.
RelativesObligate aerobes, no sugar fermentation, opportunists. B. cepacia: cystic fibrosis copathogen, mutates fast. B. pseudomallei: MELIOIDOSIS, tropical soil, injury or inhalation. A. baumannii: MONTHS on fomites, combat wounds. S. maltophilia: biofilms on equipment.

L14 · Brucella, Francisella, Bordetella, Legionella

TermWhat you need to know
BrucellaTiny coccobacilli. B. abortus (CATTLE), B. suis (PIGS). Malta / UNDULANT / Bang fever: fever fluctuating weeks to a year. Slaughtering, placentas, UNPASTEURIZED dairy. Agglutination titer. ANIMAL vaccine.
Francisella tularensisTULAREMIA, rabbit fever. Animals, water and dust, vector bites; 10–50 organisms. Ulcerative skin lesion + swollen nodes. Intracellular → RELAPSE. Attenuated vaccine.
Bordetella pertussisMinute encapsulated coccobacillus. Healthy CARRIERS; contact or aerosols. Binds and destroys CILIATED cells → mucus blocks airways.
Pertussis stagesCATARRHAL (drainage, sneezing, occasional cough) → PAROXYSMAL (persistent cough + the WHOOP).
Pertussis preventionDTaP (diphtheria, tetanus and acellular pertussis): ACELLULAR, toxoid + antigens, 5 doses 2 months–6 years. Rising: infants under 2 months unvaccinable · WANING immunity · pertactin-negative strains.
LegionellaWEAKLY Gram negative, pleomorphic. Hard to culture: CHARCOAL YEAST EXTRACT agar. Lives with AMEBAS in water. AEROSOLS (cooling towers, fountains). Legionnaires disease, Pontiac fever. Men over 50, smokers, nosocomial elderly.

L14 · The Enterics: Traits, Media, Antigens

TermWhat you need to know
Family traitsSmall non-spore-forming rods · FACULTATIVE anaerobes · ALL ferment GLUCOSE · nitrate → nitrite · OXIDASE − · catalase + · all have O antigen.
Coliform vs notCOLIFORMS ferment LACTOSE; non-coliforms do not.
Two kinds of diarrheaTOXIGENIC: on the surface, oversecretion of fluid (enterotoxigenic E. coli). INVASIVE: enter the lining, bleeding (Shigella).
MediaEMB (eosin methylene blue) and MacConkey's (crystal violet + BILE SALTS): both SELECTIVE for Gram negatives, DIFFERENTIAL for lactose fermenters (pink on MacConkey's).
AntigensH = FLAGELLAR · K = CAPSULE/fimbrial (Vi in Salmonella) · O = SOMATIC cell wall, all have it.
HospitalsWith Pseudomonas, almost HALF of nosocomial infections; soap dishes, sinks, catheters, tracheal cannulas.

L14 · Escherichia coli Pathotypes

TermWhat you need to know
E. coliCommon AEROBIC gut bacterium, but NOT the most prevalent (anaerobic Bacteroides, Bifidobacterium are). Virulence by PLASMID transfer.
EnterotoxigenicHeat-labile + heat-stable TOXINS → fluid secretion; fimbriae, SMALL intestine. TRAVELER'S DIARRHEA. Fluids; bismuth salicylate counters the toxin.
EnteroinvasiveNO toxins, mechanical damage; LARGE intestine, like SHIGELLOSIS. Bloody diarrhea, high fever.
EnteropathogenicAdheres and destroys MICROVILLI. Wasting INFANTILE diarrhea, top infant killer worldwide. Prevention: BREAST FEEDING.
Enterohemorrhagic O157:H7SHIGA toxin binds RIBOSOMES. CATTLE reservoir: beef, water, vegetables; ~100 cells. Hemorrhagic colitis, HEMOLYTIC UREMIC SYNDROME. RAINBOW agar: BLACK colonies (glucuronidase −). Antibiotics may WORSEN it; dialysis, plasmapheresis.
Outside the gutMost URINARY TRACT INFECTIONS (women, catheters) · neonatal meningitis · COLIFORM COUNT = fecal contamination of water.

L14 · Salmonella, Shigella, Enteric Yersinia

TermWhat you need to know
Salmonella typhiHUMANS the only host · resists BILE and dyes · fecal food or water (“boil it, cook it, peel it, or forget it”) · chronic GALLBLADDER carriers.
Typhoid courseSMALL intestine → invasive → SEPTICEMIA → liver, spleen; ulceration, PERFORATION. Sustained fever 103–104, ROSE SPOTS. Vaccines: oral (4 doses), injected (1).
Animal salmonellosesLess severe than typhoid but MORE PREVALENT. Ingestion: POULTRY and EGGS, cattle, REPTILES (pet turtles), backyard flocks. S. cholerae-suis = swine.

L14 · Yersinia pestis & Plague

TermWhat you need to know
OrganismTiny rod, BIPOLAR staining, capsule · COAGULASE, endotoxin, murine toxin. ~200 rodent reservoirs; United States cases WEST of the Mississippi.
The fleaCoagulase CLOTS block the flea's esophagus → RAVENOUS, bites again → REGURGITATES bacteria into the bite. 3–50 bacilli.
Sylvatic vs urbanSYLVATIC = wild animals · URBAN = domestic or semidomestic animals · human to human rare.
Three formsBUBONIC: lymph node necrosis, the BUBO (groin, axilla) · SEPTICEMIC: purpura → gangrene, the BLACK plague · PNEUMONIC: lungs, highly contagious by AEROSOL.
Diagnose fastDeath within 2 days of symptoms. History, symptoms, ASPIRATE THE BUBO. Killed or attenuated vaccine (military, vets, lab). QUARANTINE + rodent control.

L14 · Pasteurella & Haemophilus

TermWhat you need to know

Haemophilus“Blood loving,” fastidious. CHOCOLATE agar (cooked blood): needs HEMIN, nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD) or its phosphate.

L14 · Objective 2: How We Get Them

TermWhat you need to know
Water, soil, equipmentPseudomonas (biofilms on ventilators, intravenous lines) · B. pseudomallei (injury, inhalation) · Acinetobacter (fomites) · Stenotrophomonas (biofilms).
AnimalsBrucella (slaughter, unpasteurized dairy) · Francisella (rabbits, water, dust, vectors) · O157:H7 (cattle) · animal salmonelloses (poultry, eggs, reptiles) · Pasteurella (bites) · plague (fleas, rodents).
AirBordetella (carriers, aerosols) · Legionella (water aerosols) · pneumonic plague (aerosol).
Fecal-oralE. coli diarrheal strains, Salmonella typhi (humans only), Shigella.
Self and partnerE. coli urinary tract infection (women, catheters) · H. parainfluenzae (own oral flora, dental work) · H. ducreyi (sexual) · H. aegyptius (fingers, insects).

L15 · Fungi as Pathogens

TermWhat you need to know
True (primary) pathogenInvades and grows in a HEALTHY host. Can cause systemic, subcutaneous, cutaneous and superficial mycoses. The four true pathogens are thermally dimorphic.
OpportunistLittle or no virulence of its own; needs IMPAIRED defenses (human immunodeficiency virus infection, cancer, diabetes, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, autoimmune disorder, nosocomial). Once 'harmless' skin or air contaminants.
Thermal dimorphismMOLD (multicellular) at 30 °C in soil → YEAST (unicellular) at 37 °C in the body. The true pathogen's key adaptation.
Mostly harmlessRelatively nonpathogenic: ~300 of ~100,000 species cause disease. But fungi are the most common PLANT pathogens (potato blights, famines).
EmergingMycoses ~10% of nosocomial infections and rising; dermatophytes may be becoming true pathogens; invasive fungal disease rising in the immunocompromised.
Host defenseSkin, mucous membranes, cilia; MOST IMPORTANT = cell-mediated immunity (T cells, cytokines), phagocytosis, inflammation. Reinfection highly likely with opportunists.
Communicable?Most fungal infections are NOT (no host needed for the life cycle). Exceptions: dermatophytes and Candida (person to person, fomites).
Most prevalentDermatophytoses: the most common fungal diseases; often undiagnosed or misdiagnosed as allergy or autoimmune disease.

L15 · Diagnosis & Control

TermWhat you need to know
DiagnosisMicroscopy of stained specimens + culture on selective and enriched media + biochemical or serological tests.
Sabouraud dextrose agarCommonly used; its LOW pH inhibits most bacteria but not fungi (chemicals added to block bacteria).
Potassium hydroxideLyses human epithelial cells, leaves fungi intact: fungi have a THICKER cell wall.
Flowchart cluesWood's light on hairs · negative stain for a capsule · GERM TUBE test (yeasts in serum 2–4 h sprout hyphal tubes) · culture at 25–30 °C AND 37 °C to demonstrate dimorphism · long incubations (up to 8 weeks; 4 weeks for hair, skin, nails) · animal inoculation only when other methods fail.
Stains (slide 18)Aspergillus = BRANCHED SEPTATE hyphae in sputum; germinating Candida albicans = young hypha from a yeast.
No vaccinesImmunization typically ineffective because REINFECTION is exceedingly common.
ControlDrugs: amphotericin B, flucytosine, azoles, nystatin · surgical removal of damaged tissue · prevention = respirator masks, protective clothing, hygiene.
Amphotericin BDrug of choice for many infections; from Streptomyces (a soil bacterium); complexes with fungal membrane LIPIDS → leakage; TOXIC because fungi and humans are both eukaryotes (fatigue, weakness, fever, arrhythmia): 'ampho-terrible'.

L15 · The True Pathogens (Systemic)

TermWhat you need to know
Shared patternEndemic regions; soil or animal waste with conidia is DISTURBED → spores INHALED → yeasts in the lungs → may go systemic; can also inoculate broken skin.
Histoplasma capsulatumOHIO VALLEY FEVER; the MOST COMMON true pathogen; eastern/central United States, Latin America, Africa; moist nitrogen-rich soil with BIRD AND BAT GUANO; inhaled microconidia; NOT person to person; yeast multiplies inside phagocytes; most recover.
Coccidioides immitisVALLEY FEVER; southwestern United States to Mexico, Central/South America; alkaline, salty soil of semiarid, hot climates; dust (farming, construction, digs, dust storms); drought-rain-wind cycles; ARTHROCONIDIA → SPHERULES with endospores; risk: African descent, age 40–60+, dust exposure; most need no treatment.
Blastomyces dermatitidisNORTH AMERICAN blastomycosis; forest soil, decaying wood, manure; Midwest/Southeast and southern Canada; inhaled conidia → yeasts; MIMICS LUNG CANCER on chest X-ray; dogs, cats, horses; chronic skin nodules, later bone and nervous system.
Paracoccidioides brasiliensisCentral/South America (Brazil); the RAREST primary mycosis; inhalation (lung) or inoculation (skin); rural farm workers, poor nutrition, male, over 40; usually mild, self-limiting.

L15 · Subcutaneous, Cutaneous, Superficial

TermWhat you need to know
Subcutaneous mycosesFungus pushed into TRAUMATIZED skin; inhibited by the warmth of blood and viscera, so rarely disseminate; progressive tissue destruction.
Sporothrix schenckiiROSE-GARDENER'S disease: thorn, wood, bare roots; saprobe in soil; garden centers, nurseries, pine straw, hay; LYMPHOCUTANEOUS = nodule then spread to nearby lymph nodes; heat packs, super-saturated potassium iodide; systemic: amphotericin B and/or itraconazole.
DermatophytesKeratin ONLY (skin, hair, scalp, nails) = ringworm/tinea; Trichophyton, Microsporum, Epidermophyton (39 species); agent varies case to case; reservoirs humans, animals, soil; hardy spores + abraded skin + close contact or fomites; long infection, then inflammation and allergy to fungal proteins.
★ Latin namesThe deck: 'Focus on the Latin names for each body part/region' → capitis, barbae, corporis, cruris, pedis, manuum, unguium (table above).
Tinea treatmentTopical AZOLES for several weeks ± oral antifungals; debridement; ultraviolet light.
Superficial mycosesTinea VERSICOLOR = Malassezia furfur, a YEAST (scaling, mottling). WHITE piedra = Trichosporon (pubic/axillary hair). BLACK piedra = Piedraia (scalp hairs). Piedras non-contagious, hygiene; rare in the United States.

L15 · The Opportunists

TermWhat you need to know
Candida albicansThe DOMINANT opportunist; normal flora (mouth, genitalia, large intestine, skin); ~70% of nosocomial fungal infections; usually endogenous, but spread by surgery, childbirth, breast-feeding, sex; off-white pasty colony smelling of BAKING BREAD.
Candida formsTHRUSH (thick white adherent growth) · vulvovaginal (to unprotected partners, esp. uncircumcised males) · cutaneous in moist skin and burns · intertriginous (obesity). Diagnosis: budding yeast + PSEUDOHYPHAE; species by selective differential media.
Candida aurisEmerging invasive candidiasis; HOSPITAL OUTBREAKS hard to eradicate (drug resistant, thermoresistant, partly disinfectant resistant); first found in an EAR infection, Japan 2009.
Cryptococcus neoformans#1 CRITICAL on the World Health Organization list; ENCAPSULATED yeast in soil around urban PIGEON roosts; healthy people resist; lungs → MENINGES and brain; NEGATIVE STAIN; serology and biochemistry best; amphotericin B + fluconazole.
Pneumocystis jiroveciiUnicellular fungus; the MOST PROMINENT opportunistic infection in acquired immunodeficiency syndrome; also kidney transplant; widespread silent exposure (antibody titers); secretions block breathing; PENTAMIDINE + CO-TRIMOXAZOLE (not antifungals); a fungus by genetics.
Aspergillus fumigatusAirborne soil mold; azole resistance from CROP SPRAYING; #3 critical; lungs → FUNGAL BALLS; sinuses, ear canals, eyelids, conjunctiva; invasive necrotic pneumonia, brain, heart; leukemia, transplant, cystic fibrosis.
MucormycosisZygomycota (Rhizopus, Mucor) saprobes; 'black mold' in India (steroids, uncontrolled DIABETES); nose, eyes, heart, brain in diabetes, leukemia, malnutrition; kwashiorkor gut form; cut out tissue + amphotericin B.
Fusarium · eumycetomaFusarium: tropical soil mold; contaminated CONTACT LENSES, toenails, burns. Eumycetoma: poor countries; male, 11–30, farmer, no shoes; long-term drugs, amputation.

L15 · Allergies, Toxins & How We Get Them

TermWhat you need to know
AllergiesSpores → atopic allergy, seasonal allergy, asthma; farmer's lung, teapicker's lung, bark stripper's disease.
MycotoxicosesIngested or inhaled fungal toxins: AFLATOXIN (liver, cancer co-factor; grains, corn, peanuts; kills turkeys) · ERGOT (rye, Claviceps, lysergic acid; Salem) · STACHYBOTRYS (sick building, 'black mold'; not mucormycosis).
Objective 2: inhaledSystemic true pathogens (disturbed soil, guano, dust); Cryptococcus (pigeon roost soil); Aspergillus and Zygomycota (air).
Objective 2: inoculatedSporothrix (thorns, wood); Paracoccidioides skin form; nail extensions (tinea unguium).
Objective 2: contactDermatophytes (people, animals, fomites, public surfaces; barbae mainly from animals) · Candida (surgery, birth, breast-feeding, sex; usually endogenous).
Not communicableMost fungi; Histoplasma explicitly NOT person to person; piedras non-contagious.

L16 · Microbes in Food

TermWhat you need to know
Three effectsBENEFICIAL (fermentation improves flavor, taste, texture) · DETRIMENTAL (poisoning, intoxication, illness, spoilage) · NEUTRAL (just there, eaten with the food).
BreadLeavening (carbon dioxide) · flavor and aroma (organic acids, alcohols) · conditioning (enzymes on gluten). Saccharomyces cerevisiae; some Clostridium; Lactobacillus sanfrancisco (sourdough).
Beer and wineGlucose + yeast → 2 ethanol + 2 carbon dioxide. Brewing since 6000 BCE; malting, mashing, wort + hops, lagering, filtration. Wine from grapes or other fruit. Yeast source can be human SALIVA (chewed plant material).
Pickling, soySalt brine, lactic acid, vinegar, spices; soybeans → tofu, soy sauce; fish sauce, mole, tea, coffee.
MilkSTERILE until it exits the mammal; microbes from the body and milking UTENSILS; nutrient dense. First fermented dairy = accidental skin/nose/mouth microbes. Cheese: yeast, bacteria, molds. Yogurt: Lactobacillus + Streptococcus; kefir, koumiss, buttermilk.
Microbes as foodSpirulina (a cyanobacterium) in smoothies and gummies; single cell protein researched; feed additives already used for animals.
Fungi in foodRarely cause disease; mushrooms, PENICILLIN, yeast fermentation; spoil bread, cereals, acidic juices.

L16 · The Problem & the First Split

TermWhat you need to know
Scale~250 foodborne pathogens; BACTERIA most common; also viruses, parasites, microbial toxins; under-reported (slide figures: 75 million cases, 5000+ deaths).
Who watchesSurveillance + outbreak investigation: local/state health departments, Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, Food and Drug Administration, United States Department of Agriculture. ENFORCEMENT and policy: Food and Drug Administration + United States Department of Agriculture.
SymptomsDiarrhea, abdominal cramps, nausea, fever, vomiting.
IntoxicationPREFORMED TOXIN eaten → target organs. Staphylococcal toxin, Clostridium botulinum toxin.
InfectionLIVE CELLS eaten; improper cooking lets them MULTIPLY in the food → intestinal infection. Salmonella, Listeria.
The seven bacteriaE. coli, Salmonella, Campylobacter jejuni, Listeria monocytogenes, Clostridium, Bacillus cereus, Vibrio.

L16 · E. coli, Salmonella, Campylobacter

TermWhat you need to know
Escherichia coliFECAL INDICATOR organism (human and animal feces); EMB (eosin methylene blue) plates confirm it in food and water; not always pathogenic; toxin strains (enterotoxigenic, enterohemorrhagic); O157:H7 among the worst.
E. coli outbreaksFecally contaminated MEATS or VEGETABLES: hamburgers, bagged spinach, romaine lettuce, cookie dough, ground beef, raw milk cheese (western United States, 2026).
SalmonellaAnimals, mainly POULTRY and AQUATIC REPTILES; gastroenteritis, enteric fever, septicemia, shock, kidney dysfunction; incubation HOURS to WEEKS; young children and the elderly at greatest risk.
Typhoid feverSalmonella typhi: the MOST SEVERE enteric fever; invades the intestinal wall → blood and lymph nodes. 'Typhoid Mary' (Mary Mallon), the cook who persisted.
Salmonella sourcesPoultry, EGGS, PET FOOD, live backyard poultry, PEANUT BUTTER, sushi-grade seafood; produce recalls (watermelon, onions, cantaloupe, cucumbers, jalapenos).
Campylobacter jejuniA LEADING cause of bacterial diarrhea in the United States, esp. children; rapid fever, headache, dizziness, BLOODY diarrhea; infectious dose <500 cells (ONE DROP of raw poultry fluid); under-reported.

L16 · Listeria, Clostridium, B. cereus, Vibrio

TermWhat you need to know
Listeria monocytogenesBirds, mammals, SOIL; big recalls (ice cream, cantaloupe, frozen vegetables); PREGNANT women, unborn and premature babies, elderly, immunocompromised; slide: #1 cause of food-related DEATHS in the United States.
Listeria foodsSoft cheeses, deli meats, hot dogs, raw produce, raw meat, smoked seafood. Grows from 1 to 40 °C: FRIDGE, room and freezer temperatures. Spreads to brain and across the placenta to the fetus.
ClostridiumGram-positive rods, STRICT ANAEROBES, SPORES (soil, plants, HONEY). Food: botulinum, perfringens. Not food: tetani, difficile.
BotulismToxin made when spores GERMINATE; foodborne and wound botulism; paralysis with muscles RELAXED → respiratory failure; a few bacteria make plenty of toxin.
Bacillus cereusVery common, SUMMER; DIARRHEAL (meat) or EMETIC (rice); onset ≤6 hours; self-resolving vomiting and cramps; hot foods hot, cold foods cold.
Vibriocholerae, vulnificus, parahaemolyticus; SALT-tolerant, ocean and estuarine water; WATER (ingested, WOUNDS) or FOOD (shellfish, oysters, crabs, raw seafood); the R-month rule is now uncertain.

L16 · Protozoa, Worms, Viruses

TermWhat you need to know
ProtozoaCryptosporidium, Cyclospora, Giardia, Entamoeba: mild, self-limiting diarrhea from uncooked fruit and vegetables or DRINKING WATER; ready-to-eat SALAD BAGS.
WormsTapeworm, roundworm, trichinosis: killed by THOROUGH COOKING; venison and pork infected while the animal is ALIVE; pet feces.
NorovirusNorwalk-like, Caliciviridae; person to person by FECES and VOMITUS (daycares, nursing homes, CRUISE SHIPS); shellfish; a FEW particles infect; rapid onset, short course.
Hepatitis A and EFECAL-ORAL; most contagious ~2 WEEKS BEFORE symptoms; NOT kissing, sneezing, saliva or blood; vaccine after suspected exposure; handwashing, diaper hygiene.
