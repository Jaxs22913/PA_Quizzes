L14 · Endotoxin & Sorting

TermWhat you need to know
EndotoxinLIPOPOLYSACCHARIDE of the outer membrane. LIPID A = biologically active part. Released on LYSIS (including by antibiotics) and during rapid growth.
Three partsLipid A (active) · core polysaccharide (same for large groups) · O antigen (unique per organism; repeat number = rough, semi-rough, smooth).
Usually protectiveInnate immunity via a lipopolysaccharide binding complex + Toll-like receptor → moderated inflammation keeps mucosal infection mucosal.

Low vs floodLow: fever, malaise, aches. Flood: SEPTIC SHOCK — microthrombi, vasodilation, cardiac output first UP (warm shock), then down → organ failure.

Pneumocystis jiroveciiUnicellular fungus; the MOST PROMINENT opportunistic infection in acquired immunodeficiency syndrome; also kidney transplant; widespread silent exposure (antibody titers); secretions block breathing; PENTAMIDINE + CO-TRIMOXAZOLE (not antifungals); a fungus by genetics.
Aspergillus fumigatusAirborne soil mold; azole resistance from CROP SPRAYING; #3 critical; lungs → FUNGAL BALLS; sinuses, ear canals, eyelids, conjunctiva; invasive necrotic pneumonia, brain, heart; leukemia, transplant, cystic fibrosis.
MucormycosisZygomycota (Rhizopus, Mucor) saprobes; 'black mold' in India (steroids, uncontrolled DIABETES); nose, eyes, heart, brain in diabetes, leukemia, malnutrition; kwashiorkor gut form; cut out tissue + amphotericin B.
Fusarium · eumycetomaFusarium: tropical soil mold; contaminated CONTACT LENSES, toenails, burns. Eumycetoma: poor countries; male, 11–30, farmer, no shoes; long-term drugs, amputation.

L16 · Microbes in Food

TermWhat you need to know
Three effectsBENEFICIAL (fermentation improves flavor, taste, texture) · DETRIMENTAL (poisoning, intoxication, illness, spoilage) · NEUTRAL (just there, eaten with the food).
BreadLeavening (carbon dioxide) · flavor and aroma (organic acids, alcohols) · conditioning (enzymes on gluten). Saccharomyces cerevisiae; some Clostridium; Lactobacillus sanfrancisco (sourdough).
Beer and wineGlucose + yeast → 2 ethanol + 2 carbon dioxide. Brewing since 6000 BCE; malting, mashing, wort + hops, lagering, filtration. Wine from grapes or other fruit. Yeast source can be human SALIVA (chewed plant material).

Who watchesSurveillance + outbreak investigation: local/state health departments, Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, Food and Drug Administration, United States Department of Agriculture. ENFORCEMENT and policy: Food and Drug Administration + United States Department of Agriculture.

L16 · E. coli, Salmonella, Campylobacter

TermWhat you need to know
Escherichia coliFECAL INDICATOR organism (human and animal feces); EMB (eosin methylene blue) plates confirm it in food and water; not always pathogenic; toxin strains (enterotoxigenic, enterohemorrhagic); O157:H7 among the worst.
E. coli outbreaksFecally contaminated MEATS or VEGETABLES: hamburgers, bagged spinach, romaine lettuce, cookie dough, ground beef, raw milk cheese (western United States, 2026).
SalmonellaAnimals, mainly POULTRY and AQUATIC REPTILES; gastroenteritis, enteric fever, septicemia, shock, kidney dysfunction; incubation HOURS to WEEKS; young children and the elderly at greatest risk.
Typhoid feverSalmonella typhi: the MOST SEVERE enteric fever; invades the intestinal wall → blood and lymph nodes. 'Typhoid Mary' (Mary Mallon), the cook who persisted.
Salmonella sourcesPoultry, EGGS, PET FOOD, live backyard poultry, PEANUT BUTTER, sushi-grade seafood; produce recalls (watermelon, onions, cantaloupe, cucumbers, jalapenos).
Campylobacter jejuniA LEADING cause of bacterial diarrhea in the United States, esp. children; rapid fever, headache, dizziness, BLOODY diarrhea; infectious dose <500 cells (ONE DROP of raw poultry fluid); under-reported.

L16 · Listeria, Clostridium, B. cereus, Vibrio

TermWhat you need to know
Listeria monocytogenesBirds, mammals, SOIL; big recalls (ice cream, cantaloupe, frozen vegetables); PREGNANT women, unborn and premature babies, elderly, immunocompromised; slide: #1 cause of food-related DEATHS in the United States.
Listeria foodsSoft cheeses, deli meats, hot dogs, raw produce, raw meat, smoked seafood. Grows from 1 to 40 °C: FRIDGE, room and freezer temperatures. Spreads to brain and across the placenta to the fetus.
ClostridiumGram-positive rods, STRICT ANAEROBES, SPORES (soil, plants, HONEY). Food: botulinum, perfringens. Not food: tetani, difficile.
BotulismToxin made when spores GERMINATE; foodborne and wound botulism; paralysis with muscles RELAXED → respiratory failure; a few bacteria make plenty of toxin.
Bacillus cereusVery common, SUMMER; DIARRHEAL (meat) or EMETIC (rice); onset ≤6 hours; self-resolving vomiting and cramps; hot foods hot, cold foods cold.
Vibriocholerae, vulnificus, parahaemolyticus; SALT-tolerant, ocean and estuarine water; WATER (ingested, WOUNDS) or FOOD (shellfish, oysters, crabs, raw seafood); the R-month rule is now uncertain.

L17 · Parasites, Protozoa & the Amebas

TermWhat you need to know
ParasitologyStudy of eukaryotic parasites: PROTOZOA and HELMINTHS. Parasites cause 20% of all infectious diseases; less prevalent in industrialized countries; increasingly common with human immunodeficiency virus infection and other immunocompromise.
Neglected parasitic infectionsCenters for Disease Control and Prevention priorities (people infected, severity, ability to prevent and treat): CHAGAS DISEASE, CYSTICERCOSIS, TOXOCARIASIS, TOXOPLASMOSIS, TRICHOMONIASIS.
ProtozoaSingle-celled, animal-like, most motile: AMEBOID, CILIATED, FLAGELLATED, APICOMPLEXA. ~100,000 species, ~25 important pathogens. Most divide asexually as the TROPHOZOITE (active feeding cell); many form a CYST; some have asexual and sexual phases. World Health Organization 2014: “Small bite, big threat” (vector-borne disease).
★ Antiprotozoan drugsRelatively FEW compared with antibacterial agents ★. They work against EUKARYOTIC cells; toxicity problems, severe side effects ★.
Entamoeba histolyticaLarge trophozoite (PSEUDOPODS, large nucleus, few organelles) + smaller nonmotile CYST. Histo-lytica = tissue lysis. Humans the primary host; carried by 10% of the world, asymptomatic. Cysts SWALLOWED (fecal-oral); in the small intestine each releases 4 trophozoites. Tropical; polluted water in prisons, hospitals, juvenile care; sometimes sexually transmitted.
Amebiasis90% asymptomatic. INTESTINAL: cecum, appendix, colon, rectum; dysentery, pain, fever, diarrhea, weight loss; hemorrhage, perforation, appendicitis, AMEBOMAS. EXTRAINTESTINAL: the LIVER most often (abscess, amebic hepatitis); lungs, spleen, adrenals, kidneys, skin, brain; severe forms 10% fatal.
★ Entamoeba: diagnosis, preventionLooks like bacillary dysentery: fecal smear, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay with monoclonal antibodies, history. ★ WATER TREATMENT is the best prevention; a vaccine is under development.
★ Naegleria fowleriStanding water with a high bacterial count: hot springs, lakes, streams, unchlorinated pools (Florida’s freshwater lakes). NASAL contact with water (diving, waterskiing, jetskiing, sinus rinsing even with tap water) → nasal mucosa → OLFACTORY NERVE → brain. Primary amebic meningoencephalitis: children and young adults; headache, fever, stiff neck → coma, death in 3–7 days. Nose plugs; no unchlorinated water. ★ Treatment must be EARLY.
AcanthamoebaWorldwide: freshwater lakes, hot tubs, pools. AMEBIC KERATITIS in CONTACT LENS wearers (eye pain, redness; does not typically disseminate). GRANULOMATOUS AMEBIC ENCEPHALITIS through a skin break or the nose: meningitis symptoms, LONGER progression, survival only 2–3%. Drugs only tentatively successful.

L17 · Balantidium, Trichomonas, Giardia

TermWhat you need to know
Balantidium coliThe only CILIATE. Lives in the intestines of PIGS and CATTLE; cysts in food or water. The trophozoite erodes the intestine: persistent diarrhea, occasionally dysentery, pain, weight loss. HEALTHY HUMANS ARE RESISTANT; rarely penetrates the intestine or enters the blood.
TrichomonadsSmall, PEAR-SHAPED; 4 anterior flagella + an UNDULATING MEMBRANE. Trophozoite only: NO CYST. Three infect humans: Trichomonas vaginalis, tenax, hominis.
★ Trichomonas vaginalis★ One you will meet in clinical practice. Causes TRICHOMONIASIS, the most common CURABLE sexually transmitted disease in young, sexually active women. Reservoir: the human urogenital tract; a STRICT parasite that cannot survive long outside the host; 60–70% asymptomatic.
TrichomoniasisWomen: FOUL-SMELLING, GREEN-TO-YELLOW discharge, vulvitis, cervicitis, urinary frequency and pain. Men: urethritis, thin milky discharge, occasionally prostate. Incubation 4–28 days. Diagnosis: microscopy of a VAGINAL SMEAR. Predisposes to other sexually transmitted diseases; cervical cancer risk ×2–3; human immunodeficiency virus transmission +50%.
Giardia lamblia: formFlagellate with a symmetrical HEART shape; the concave ventral surface acts as a SUCTION CUP. Cysts small, compact, multinucleate; survive 2 months in the environment.
★ Giardia lamblia: source★ The one you get CAMPING (slide 4: common in campers). Reservoirs: beavers, cattle, coyotes, cats, humans. Cysts ingested with water and food; infectious dose 10–100 cysts.
GiardiasisCysts enter the duodenum, germinate, feed and multiply in the jejunum: diarrhea, abdominal pain. Shed in feces INTERMITTENTLY, so microscopy is difficult: POLYMERASE CHAIN REACTION. Killed by BOILING, OZONE, IODINE.

L17 · Trypanosomes & Leishmania

TermWhat you need to know
HemoflagellatesObligate blood and tissue parasites; life-threatening zoonoses spread in tropical regions by BLOOD-SUCKING INSECTS (intermediate hosts); complicated life cycles, sorted by cellular and infective stages.
★ Trypanosoma: the pictureElongate, SPINDLE-SHAPED cell, tapered ends, EEL-LIKE motility. ★ A blood smear with wavy flagellates swimming BETWEEN red blood cells (slides 5, 46) = Trypanosoma.
Trypanosoma bruceiAfrican SLEEPING SICKNESS; TSETSE FLY; reservoir mammals. Gambiense: WEST Africa, CHRONIC (brain years later). Rhodesiense: EAST Africa, ACUTE (brain within 3–4 weeks). Bite → PRIMARY CHANCRE → lymphatics → blood. ANTIGENIC VARIATION (over 100 antigens) evades the immune response.
Sleeping sickness: course, diagnosisFever, enlarged spleen, swollen lymph nodes, joint pain → central nervous system: personality change, SLEEP DISTURBANCES, tremors, seizures, paralysis, coma. Readily seen in blood, spinal fluid, lymph nodes; CARD AGGLUTINATION TEST for trypanosomes = rapid diagnosis.
★ Trypanosoma cruziCHAGAS DISEASE (American trypanosomiasis): Central and South America; ★ becoming an issue in the SOUTHERN UNITED STATES through VECTOR MIGRATION (starred on slide 48); now considered endemic in the United States by many.
Chagas: how it spreadsREDUVIID (KISSING) BUG: its FECES are inoculated into a cutaneous portal. Also congenital, transfusion, organ transplant, food contaminated by triatomine urine or feces (more severe, higher mortality).
Chagas diseaseROMAÑA’S SIGN (chagoma): eyelid swelling with conjunctivitis; fever; swollen lymph nodes, spleen, liver. Masses of the parasite in HEART MUSCLE and LARGE INTESTINE; chronic heart and brain inflammation (30–40% of heart disease in endemic regions).
★ Controlling the hemoflagellates★ VECTOR CONTROL is the best way: eliminate the tsetse fly; control the reduviid bug (Chagas vaccine under development). ★ Treat EARLY: Trypanosoma brucei before neurological involvement; Trypanosoma cruzi early.
LeishmaniaFemale SAND FLY (needs a blood meal to make eggs); DOGS alternate hosts; equatorial. Injected with the bite; LOCALIZED if the macrophage stays, SYSTEMIC if it migrates. Cutaneous (oriental sore, BAGHDAD BOIL) → ESPUNDIA (head skin and mucous membranes) → VISCERAL (fever, enlarged spleen, liver, nodes); KALA AZAR the most severe, fatal untreated. Co-infection with malaria confuses diagnosis.

L17 · Malaria & Babesiosis

TermWhat you need to know
ApicomplexansSporozoans: NO LOCOMOTOR ORGANELLES in the trophozoite; alternate sexual and asexual phases and different animal hosts; specialized infective bodies spread by arthropod vectors, food, water or other means (Plasmodium, Toxoplasma, Cryptosporidium).
PlasmodiumObligate INTRACELLULAR sporozoan. Four species: Plasmodium malariae, vivax, falciparum, ovale. Malaria is the DOMINANT protozoan disease: threatens 40% of the world’s population; 90% of cases in Africa.
Malaria: spreadFemale ANOPHELES mosquito (primary vector); also blood transfusion and mother to fetus.
In the human (asexual)Sporozoites → LIVER (schizogony) → merozoites into the blood in 5–16 days (exoerythrocytic) → into RED BLOOD CELLS as RING trophozoites, multiply, burst (erythrocytic) → gametes in the blood (gametocytic).
In the mosquito (sexual)The female Anopheles draws infected red cells; gametes fertilize → diploid cell → sporozoites in the stomach → SALIVARY GLANDS, ready to infect.
Malaria: diseaseIncubation 10–16 days; malaise, fatigue, aches, nausea; regular CHILLS-FEVER-SWEATING every 48–72 hours as red cells rupture (interval depends on species). Anemia; enlarged spleen, liver, kidneys. Plasmodium FALCIPARUM the most malignant, highest death rate in children. Diagnosis: trophozoites (rings) in red cells + symptoms.
Malaria: resistance, controlSICKLE CELL TRAIT = natural resistance. Increasing drug resistance. ★ MOSQUITO CONTROL IS KEY. ★ Non-travel cases now seen in Florida. World Health Organization vaccine for children: complex regimen before rainy seasons, about 30% effective.
BabesiaThe FIRST protozoan found to cause a disease (REDWATER FEVER of cattle) and the FIRST linked to a vector (the TICK). Human babesiosis: Babesia microti, BLACKLEGGED DEER TICK; rare zoonosis; flu-like, attacks red cells → HEMOLYTIC ANEMIA. Serious without a SPLEEN, if immunocompromised, with liver or kidney disease, or over 50.

L17 · Toxoplasma & the Gut Coccidians

TermWhat you need to know
CoccidiansZoonotic in domestic animals and birds; dormant OOCYSTS and pseudocysts. Toxoplasma, Sarcocystis, Cryptosporidium, Isospora, Cyclospora, Babesia.
Toxoplasma gondiiIntracellular; infects virtually all warm-blooded animals; about one third of people exposed. CATS (the feline definitive host) shed OOCYSTS in feces. Acquired from RAW MEAT or anything contaminated by CAT FECES.
★ Toxoplasma: pregnancyTRANSPLACENTAL (33%): spontaneous abortion, stillbirth, HYDROCEPHALUS, INTRACRANIAL CALCIFICATIONS, CHORIORETINITIS. ★ Pregnant women should not clean the cat box.
Toxoplasma: everyone elseMost cases unnoticed (sore throat, fever, enlarged lymph nodes). Immunocompromised, acquired immunodeficiency syndrome: brain and heart damage, TOXOPLASMA ENCEPHALITIS. Diagnosis: serology, culture, histology. Linked to schizophrenia and mood disorders; infected hyena cubs grow bolder near lions.
SarcocystisCattle, swine, sheep are intermediate hosts (they graze on grass contaminated with human feces). Humans eat UNDERCOOKED MEAT: Sarcocystis hominis (cattle), Sarcocystis suihominis (pigs) → diarrhea, nausea, pain; other species → muscle cysts, myalgia, weakness, edema.
CryptosporidiumIntestinal; mammals, birds, reptiles; tissue and oocyst phases. RECREATIONAL WATER, water, ice, food; TOLERATES POOL CHLORINE; FILTRATION needed (Milwaukee: 370,000 cases). Infectious dose ~10. ★ Leading cause of recreational waterborne disease; potential bioterrorism agent.
CryptosporidiosisSporozoites penetrate intestinal cells: gastroenteritis, cramps, diarrhea, vomiting. Acquired immunodeficiency syndrome and other immunocompromised: CHRONIC PERSISTENT diarrhea; young children, elderly. Few effective drugs.
Isospora belliIntracellular intestinal parasite with an oocyst; fecally contaminated food or drink. Usually asymptomatic or self-limited; diarrhea, FATTY STOOLS, weight loss. Primarily IMMUNOCOMPROMISED hosts.
Cyclospora cayetanensisEmerging; oral-fecal via FRESH PRODUCE and water; oocysts invade the small-intestine mucosa: WATERY diarrhea, cramps, bloating, fever, muscle aches. Needs MULTIPLE stool samples; ACID-FAST stain. 2026 outbreak from ICEBERG LETTUCE.

L17 · Helminths: Hosts, Cycles, Defense

TermWhat you need to know
HelminthsLarge multicellular worms with specialized tissues; adults mate → eggs → larvae mature in stages. Sexes separate or hermaphroditic. Sources: contaminated food, soil, water, infected animals.
★ Definitive host★ Where ADULTHOOD and MATING occur (starred on slide 79).
Intermediate and transport hostsINTERMEDIATE: larval development. TRANSPORT (paratenic): no parasitic development; carries it to the definitive host.
Cycles A and BA: SWALLOW the mature egg (Ascaris, Trichuris); larvae may pass lungs → pharynx → swallowed; adults in the intestine. B: LARVAE in soil PENETRATE THE SKIN (hookworms, Strongyloides) → lungs → swallowed → intestine.
Cycles C and DC: EAT the ENCYSTED worm in undercooked meat (Trichinella, Taenia). D: an INTERMEDIATE HOST; larvae through the skin, eaten, or by an insect bite (filarial worms, Schistosoma, Opisthorchis, Paragonimus).
★ Adaptations, pathologyAttachment mouthparts, tissue-dissolving enzymes, protective cuticle, reduced organ systems. ★ PATHOLOGY: feeding and migration through tissue, organ blockage, toxic secretions → organ swelling, hemorrhage, weight loss, anemia.
Defense and diagnosisEOSINOPHILS defend; worms are hard to clear without drugs. Diagnosis: EOSINOPHILIA, serology; eggs, larvae or worms in feces, sputum, urine, blood or biopsies. Many antihelminthic drugs are toxic.
Nematodes (roundworms)Among the most abundant animals; 50 species affect humans. Cylindrical, protective cuticle, complete digestive tract, separate sexes. Intestinal or tissue nematodes.

L17 · Intestinal Roundworms

TermWhat you need to know
Ascaris lumbricoidesLarge intestinal ROUNDWORM; humans only; most United States cases in the SOUTHEAST. Cycle A: eggs in food, drink, objects → larvae through the gut wall → LUNGS → pharynx → swallowed → adults (200,000 eggs a day).
AscariasisWorms stay motile, do NOT attach. Inflammation along the migration route; allergy; heavy loads → pain and INTESTINAL OBSTRUCTION; lungs → EOSINOPHILIC PNEUMONITIS; may retard physical and mental development. Diagnosis: microscopic or macroscopic.
Trichuris trichiuraWHIPWORM; humans the sole host; the tropics, the United States South. Cycle A: eggs hatch in the intestine; larvae attach and penetrate. Pierce capillaries → localized HEMORRHAGE; heavy loads → dysentery, RECTAL PROLAPSE; can be fatal in children.
★ Enterobius vermicularisPINWORM (seatworm). ★ The most common worm disease of CHILDREN in temperate zones (starred on slide 94); ★ likely to be met in practice; daycare. Eggs from surroundings, close contact, fomites; SELF-INOCULATION. Females emerge to lay eggs: ANAL ITCHING. Diagnosis: TAPE TEST.
HookwormsNecator americanus (New World), Ancylostoma duodenale (Old World): curved ends, hooked mouths. Cycle B: FILARIFORM LARVAE burrow into BARE FEET → lungs → swallowed → small intestine. Pneumonia, bloody diarrhea; BLOOD LOSS → ANEMIA.
Strongyloides stercoralisTHREADWORM: tiny; lives in humans or moist soil. Cycle B: larvae through the skin → lungs → intestine. Can REINFECT THE SAME HOST without leaving it. Heavy loads: pneumonitis, eosinophilia, bloody diarrhea, liver enlargement, obstruction, malabsorption.
★ Trichinella spiralisCycle C, entirely in mammals: UNDERCOOKED PORK or BEAR meat → larvae to blood vessels, muscle, heart, brain → CYSTS. First flulike, diarrhea; then muscle and joint pain, shortness of breath, PRONOUNCED EOSINOPHILIA; myocarditis. ★ NO CURE once encysted.
Angiostrongylus cantonensisRAT LUNGWORM; SNAILS are intermediate hosts. Eat snails, or contaminated water or vegetables → EOSINOPHILIC MENINGITIS (looks like bacterial meningitis); lumbar puncture: EOSINOPHILS instead of neutrophils. Some recover; some coma and death. Control snails and slugs; wash produce.

L17 · Tissue & Zoonotic Roundworms

TermWhat you need to know
Tissue nematodesFILARIAL worms in blood, lymphatics or skin; spread by BITING ARTHROPODS (Cycle D, the vector is the intermediate host); chronic, deforming disease.
Wuchereria bancroftiMOSQUITOES; tropical. Larvae → LYMPHATICS → adults → blocked lymph flow → ELEPHANTIASIS (massive swelling of the extremities).
Onchocerca volvulusBLACK FLIES. Adults in subcutaneous tissue; females migrate to the EYES. RIVER BLINDNESS: antigens of WOLBACHIA bacteria (an endosymbiont) inflame cornea, retina, optic nerve.
Loa loaAfrican EYE WORM; small flies (mangrove or deer flies). Temperature-sensitive; migrates around and under the skin; may enter the EYE.
Toxocara canis and catiDOG and CAT roundworms; a neglected parasitic infection. Humans are ACCIDENTAL hosts: eggs from feces, or undercooked meat of a paratenic host (waterfowl). Larvae migrate: OCULAR (visual impairment, blindness) or VISCERAL (liver damage) toxocariasis.

L17 · Flukes & Tapeworms

TermWhat you need to know
Trematodes (flukes)Flatworms, ovoid and leaflike; digestive, excretory, neuromuscular, reproductive systems; NO circulatory or respiratory system. SNAILS or fish usually intermediate hosts; humans definitive.
SchistosomaBLOOD FLUKES (mansoni, japonicum, haematobium); Africa, South America, Middle East, Far East. Eggs in water → miracidium in a FRESHWATER SNAIL → cercaria PENETRATES THE SKIN → liver → adults to intestine or bladder, feed on blood; eggs in urine and feces. Itchy entry site, fever, liver, spleen, bladder inflammation. SNAIL CONTROL. (Table 23.4: ingestion of fresh water.)
Opisthorchis sinensisCHINESE LIVER FLUKE (Clonorchis); mammals, snails, fish. Inadequately cooked FISH (“don’t eat freshwater sushi”) → larvae crawl into the BILE DUCT.
Paragonimus westermaniLUNG FLUKE; carnivorous animals, snails, CRUSTACEANS. Undercooked CRABS → worms to the LUNGS: cough, pleural pain, abscess.
Fasciola hepaticaSHEEP LIVER FLUKE; herbivores, snails, aquatic plants. Raw AQUATIC PLANTS → LIVER. Acute: abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting. Chronic (months to years): blocked bile duct, gall bladder, pancreas.
Cestodes (tapeworms)Flatworms, Cycle C. Ribbonlike chain of PROGLOTTIDS (each absorbs food, makes and releases eggs) + a SCOLEX that grips the intestine. NO MOUTH: absorb through the body. Humans definitive, animals intermediate.
Taenia saginataBEEF tapeworm (Cycle C and modified A); up to 2,000 proglottids. Raw beef with encysted larvae → adult in the small intestine. Taeniasis: FEW symptoms, vague pain, nausea, PROGLOTTIDS IN STOOL (slide 8: weight loss and wasting).
Taenia soliumPORK tapeworm. Undercooked pork → TAENIASIS. Embryonated EGGS in food or water → CYSTICERCOSIS: larvae encyst in all tissues; worst in HEART, EYE, BRAIN → SEIZURES, psychiatric disturbances. A neglected parasitic infection.
EchinococcusTiny tapeworm; DOGS definitive (alveolar: foxes, coyotes, dogs); sheep, cattle, goats, pigs intermediate. From HANDLING DOGS. Cystic = HYDATID disease: slowly enlarging cysts in liver, lungs. Alveolar: parasitic tumors in lungs, liver, brain; rare, can be fatal. Reduces natural killer cell activity.

L17 · Arthropod Vectors

TermWhat you need to know
ArthropodsExoskeleton and jointed legs (includes arachnids and crustaceans). Many feed on blood and tissue fluid: ECTOPARASITES. Those of medical importance transmit microbes while feeding: BIOLOGICAL VECTORS.
MosquitoesNeed an AQUATIC habitat; FEMALES take the blood meal: malaria (Anopheles), filariasis (Anopheles/Culex), dengue fever.
FleasHighly motile, flattened; feed on warm-blooded animals; zoonoses: PLAGUE, murine typhus.
