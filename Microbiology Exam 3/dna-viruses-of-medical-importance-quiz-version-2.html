Before you begin

Lecture 18 of Microbiology — DNA Viruses of Medical Importance. Thirty questions, every one cited to the slide it came from and weighted by the minutes Dr. Webster spent on each topic (most on the phage cycles, smallpox, mpox, hepatitis B, cytomegalovirus and Epstein-Barr virus). The two sets split the smaller viruses between them; take both. Know how an animal virus replicates differently from a phage (engulfed whole, uncoated, released by budding), the three kinds of infection (acute, persistent chronic, persistent latent) with their examples, which viruses are oncogenic and which teratogenic, and for every virus how it spreads, what it causes and whether there is a vaccine. Every set asks what she stressed: animal virus versus phage replication (“this you need to know”), the kinds of viral infection, the eight human herpesviruses (“put a star on this little slide”) and hepatitis B (“you need to know that one”). No question asks which drug treats what, a date or a number.
