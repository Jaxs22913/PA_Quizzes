Before you begin

Lecture 15 of Microbiology — Fungi of Medical Importance. Thirty questions, every one cited to the slide it came from and weighted by slide count until the lecture recording has been read; every set carries the tinea names by body site (the slides say to focus on the Latin names). Start with two questions: can the fungus infect a healthy person (a true pathogen, thermally dimorphic: a mold at 30 °C, a yeast at 37 °C) or only a compromised one (an opportunist)? And how deep does it go: systemic (inhaled spores), subcutaneous (a puncture wound), cutaneous (keratin only) or superficial? The true pathogens: Histoplasma, Coccidioides, Blastomyces and Paracoccidioides, each with its region and its soil. Skin and below: Sporothrix, the tineas, tinea versicolor and the piedras. The opportunists: Candida, Cryptococcus, Pneumocystis, Aspergillus, the mucormycoses and Fusarium, then fungal allergies and toxins. Know each one's morphology, culture and tests, disease, treatment, prevention and, for objective 2, how people acquire it. No question asks for a percentage, a species count or a dose.
