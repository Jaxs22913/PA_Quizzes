Lecture 16 of Microbiology — Significant Microorganisms in Food Safety. Thirty questions, every one cited to the slide it came from and weighted by slide count until the lecture recording has been read. Microbes in food are good (bread, beer, cheese, yogurt), bad (illness and spoilage) or just there. The first split is intoxication (a preformed toxin) against infection (live cells that multiply). The seven bacteria: Escherichia coli, Salmonella, Campylobacter jejuni, Listeria monocytogenes, Clostridium, Bacillus cereus and Vibrio, each with its source, its food and who is at risk; then protozoa, worms, norovirus and hepatitis A and E. Tracking: pulsed-field gel electrophoresis patterns in PulseNet. Objective 3 (antibiotic resistance in the food supply) has no slide and is not asked. Where a slide and current references differ, the slide is keyed and the study guide says what differs.
