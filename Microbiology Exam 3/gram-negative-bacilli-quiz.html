Before you begin

Lecture 14 of Microbiology — Gram-Negative Bacilli of Medical Importance, the first lecture of Exam 3. Thirty questions, every one cited to the slide it came from. Start with what every Gram-negative rod carries: endotoxin, the lipopolysaccharide of the outer membrane, whose lipid A drives fever and, in a flood, septic shock. Then sort by oxygen, lactose and oxidase. The aerobic nonenteric rods: Pseudomonas aeruginosa (one polar flagellum, pyocyanin, biofilms, burns and cystic fibrosis) and its relatives, the zoonotic Brucella and Francisella, Bordetella pertussis and Legionella. The enterics: glucose fermenters that are oxidase negative, split into lactose-fermenting coliforms (Escherichia coli and its four pathotypes, Klebsiella, Enterobacter) and non-coliforms (Proteus, Salmonella, Shigella, Yersinia). Then Yersinia pestis and plague, Pasteurella and Haemophilus. Know each organism's shape, medium, disease, prevention and, for objective 2, how people acquire it. No question asks for a percentage, a case count, a dose or which antibiotic to give.
