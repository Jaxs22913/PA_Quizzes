Before you begin

Sixty questions drawn from every lecture in the Exam 2 block (Lectures 4 to 8), so each form is a genuine cumulative rehearsal. Weighted by the timetable: these practice exams follow the convention of five questions per scheduled lecture hour, and Lectures 4, 5, 6 and 7 were two hours each while Lecture 8 was one, so each form carries 14 ophthalmic, 13 ENT, 13 antihypertensive, 13 lipid-lowering and 7 myocardial ischemia questions. Nine hours at five per hour is 45, not 60, so the sixty are apportioned across those hours rather than invented. Questions are reused verbatim from the topic quizzes and clinical vignettes, so nothing here can drift from the material it summarizes, and no question appears in more than one form: all five give you 300 distinct questions. Weighted the way the course asked for: mechanism is capped at a fifth of each lecture, so indications, drug choice, side effects, contraindications and patient education carry the count. No question turns on a drug dose, per Dr. Wood. Answer positions are balanced inside every form (fifteen of each letter), and every wrong answer says what it actually belongs to.
