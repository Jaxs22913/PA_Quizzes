Before you begin

Mechanism only, which is the line this course draws against Clinical Medicine and Surgery: how the immune system works and how it injures belongs here, what is done about it belongs there. One idea organizes the antibody half. An antibody has two ends that do different jobs: the Fab arms recognize the antigen, and the Fc stem decides what happens next — which cell it recruits, whether complement starts, where the molecule can travel. The five classes differ in that constant region, which is why IgM agglutinates, IgG crosses the placenta, IgA guards the mucosa and IgE arms the mast cell. Hypersensitivity is sorted by mechanism, not by severity. Type I is IgE on mast cells, type II is antibody bound to a cell or tissue antigen, type III is deposited immune complexes, and type IV is sensitized T cells. Ask which of the four is doing the damage and the examples follow. Weighted by the narration.
