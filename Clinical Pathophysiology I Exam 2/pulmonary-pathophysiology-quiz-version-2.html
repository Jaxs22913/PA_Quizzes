Before you begin

Mechanism only, which is the line this course draws against Clinical Medicine and Surgery: why the lung fails belongs here, what is done about it belongs there. Sort every disease into one of two bins first. Obstructive diseases raise airflow resistance — from the airway wall (asthma, bronchitis), from lost parenchyma that held the airways open (emphysema) or from material in the lumen (bronchiectasis). Restrictive diseases stop the lungs expanding — from the parenchyma, the pleura, the chest wall or the neuromuscular pump. The bin predicts the test pattern: a low FEV1/FVC (forced expiratory volume in 1 second to forced vital capacity) ratio for obstruction, a low total lung capacity for restriction. Hypoxia drives the right heart. Hypoxic vasoconstriction raises pulmonary pressure, the right ventricle dilates, and that is cor pulmonale. Weighted by the lecture.
