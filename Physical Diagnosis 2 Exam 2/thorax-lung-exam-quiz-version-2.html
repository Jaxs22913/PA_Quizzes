Before you begin

Thirty questions on the advanced thorax and lung examination. The five conditions are told apart by four findings read together: the trachea, tactile fremitus, the percussion note and the breath sounds. Pneumonia is dull with INCREASED fremitus, bronchial sounds and egophony; pleural effusion is dull with DECREASED fremitus and sounds; pneumothorax is hyperresonant with decreased fremitus and sounds; asthma is diffusely hyperresonant with wheezes and a prolonged expiration. Watch which way the trachea goes: atelectasis pulls it toward the affected side, while a tension pneumothorax or a large effusion pushes it toward the unaffected side. Three sounds need immediate action: stridor over the neck on inspiration, the silent chest of asthma, and massive hemoptysis. A pleural rub disappears when the breath is held; a pericardial friction rub does not. Covers the landmarks and the procedures tied to them, the respiratory history (chest pain patterns, dyspnea, cough, sputum, hemoptysis and disordered sleep), clubbing, sudden against gradual or episodic dyspnea, the breathing patterns and chest wall deformities, palpation, percussion, the four breath sounds, crackles, wheezes, rhonchi and the other adventitious sounds, and the transmitted voice tests.
