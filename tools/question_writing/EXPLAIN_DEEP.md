# Deep answer-choice explanations (Jaxon, 2026-10-29)

"All answer choices should have choice rationale as to why they are correct or incorrect and good
explanations." Every choice already had its own explanation; this raises the bar on what each one
says. It applies to every new question and to rewrites of existing ones. WRITER.md still governs the
stem, options and key; this file governs the explanation text only.

## The keyed choice (2 to 4 sentences, about 200 to 400 characters)

1. Opens with `Correct.`
2. Why it is right for THIS patient: name the decisive clue(s) from the stem (age, comorbidity,
   finding, lab value against its range, timing).
3. The fact or mechanism behind it, from the slides.
4. For a contraindication or "what instead" item: why it is safe here when the obvious choice is not.

## Each wrong choice (2 to 3 sentences, about 180 to 350 characters)

1. Why it is wrong IN THIS CASE: the specific stem clue or comorbidity that rules it out, or the
   fact it misstates. Never a bare "That is X."
2. The replacing fact: what this option actually is, does, or when it WOULD be the answer.
3. One tell: how to tell it apart from the key.

## Score questions ("what is the score from this vignette")

- The keyed explanation shows the arithmetic item by item from the stem
  ("hypertension 1 + age 68 years 1 + female 1 + diabetes 1 = 4"), then what the score means or
  triggers if the slides state it.
- Each wrong number says which item it missed, double-counted or mis-weighted.
- Numeric options ascending, never shuffled.

## Always

- Slides-only: no fact the deck does not state. The explanation may restate the stem's clues.
- Never name the slide, lecture, deck, professor or syllabus. The cite field carries the source.
- No abbreviation without its full term (`ABBREV (full term)` on first use in each explanation).
- US spelling. No opening a wrong choice with "Correct".
- Do not pad: depth comes from the clue, the replacing fact and the tell, not from repetition.
